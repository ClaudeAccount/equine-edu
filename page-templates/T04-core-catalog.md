# T04 — Core Catalog Page

**File slot:** `4-<topic>.html` (or `3-` when no T03 exists) — e.g., `4-face-markings`, `3-color-types`, `4-complete-saddle-setup`
**Existing components:** `lesson-hero`, `lesson-wrap`, `tab-system`, `tab-bar`, `tab-panel`, `tab-intro`, `concept-grid`, `visual-card`, `lesson-module-tag`
**Institutional model:** This is the extension fact-sheet body — the canonical "types of X" reference (HorseQuest and Penn State structure every identification topic as a catalog of named types with photos and distinguishing features). It is also the page horse bowl members study from, so completeness and precision matter most here.

This is the main teaching page of every course: the catalog of items the learner must be able to identify or explain (colors, markings, patterns, parts, styles, equipment).

## Required sections (in order)

1. **Module objectives** — verbatim, at top.
2. **Tab intro** (per tab, 30–60 words) — what this group of items has in common, before the items.
3. **Catalog entries** — one `visual-card` per item. Each entry, in fixed order:
   - **Name** (heading)
   - **One-line identifier** — the single distinguishing feature, ≤ 15 words ("White patch between the eyes, not extending down the face").
   - **Description** — 50–150 words: what it looks like, common variations, what it can be confused with.
   - **Photo** — labeled, alt text states the distinguishing feature.
4. **Confusable callout** — after any pair of similar items, a short "How to tell them apart" box naming the one feature that separates them (discriminative contrast — Kornell & Bjork).
5. **Check Your Understanding** — 1–2 ungraded retrieval questions after every 2–3 entries (or per tab).
6. **Key Terms recap** at page end.
7. **Continue CTA** to Viewing Room.

## Content rules

- 4–7 catalog entries per tab; if a tab exceeds 7, split into two tabs or two courses (working-memory chunk limit).
- Total page: 800–1,500 words Round Pen, up to 2,000 Learning Loft.
- Identical entry structure for every item — learners should know exactly where to look for the identifier. Uniformity is the anti-helter-skelter rule.
- Use tabs to group by logical family, not to hide required content; every tab is required reading.

## Do not

- Mix "fun facts" into entries — optional material goes in a clearly labeled Further Exploration block at page end (coherence principle, Mayer).
- Vary the entry format between items or between courses.

## QA checklist

- [ ] Every entry has name, one-line identifier, description, labeled photo
- [ ] Confusable callouts present for similar pairs
- [ ] ≤ 7 entries per tab
- [ ] Retrieval checks interleaved
- [ ] Entry format identical across all tabs
