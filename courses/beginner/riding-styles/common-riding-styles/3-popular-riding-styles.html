Common Riding Styles

Popular Riding Styles

Western and English are broad riding families. Within each family are disciplines with their own goals, movement, tack, and show standards.

Think Family First, Discipline Second

Western and English describe large style families. A discipline is the specific activity or event within that family. For example, reining is a Western discipline, while dressage is an English discipline.

Explore Common Disciplines

Western

Trail Riding

Recreational or competitive riding over natural or prepared obstacles. It emphasizes calm handling, good manners, and practical movement.

Western

Ranch Riding

A style that rewards a horse that looks useful, responsive, and comfortable doing everyday ranch-type work.

Western

Western Pleasure

A judged rail class focused on a relaxed, consistent horse that moves quietly and willingly at the walk, jog, and lope.

Western

Reining

A pattern class built around controlled circles, spins, sliding stops, rollbacks, and precise transitions.

Western

Barrel Racing

A timed event where horse and rider run a cloverleaf pattern around three barrels as quickly and cleanly as possible.

Western

Cow Work

Includes events such as cutting, roping, and working cow horse, where the horse responds to cattle and rider cues.

English

Dressage

A discipline focused on precise, balanced movement and communication through a prepared test of required figures.

English

Hunters

A judged jumping discipline that values smooth rhythm, steady pace, good form, and an overall polished round.

English

Jumpers

A jumping discipline scored by speed and faults. The goal is to clear the course accurately and efficiently.

English

Eventing

A three-phase sport that combines dressage, cross-country, and stadium jumping to test versatility and fitness.

English

Equitation

A judged discipline focused on the rider's position, effectiveness, and ability to guide the horse smoothly.

English

Saddle Seat

A style often associated with high-stepping breeds and show-ring presentation, emphasizing animation and presence.
