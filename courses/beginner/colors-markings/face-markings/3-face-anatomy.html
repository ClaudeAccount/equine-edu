Face Markings

Parts of the Face

Before you can identify facial markings, it helps to know the basic parts of a horse's face where they appear. This makes it easier to understand how markings are named and described.

Face Marking Basics

Face markings are named and described by their location on the horse's face. A star is not just "a white mark" — it's a white mark specifically on the forehead. A snip is not just "a small white patch" — it's a small white patch on the muzzle between or around the nostrils.

Learning the parts of the horse's face first makes it easier to understand why each marking has its name. It also helps describe them clearly in real situations.

Parts of the Horse's Face

Work from top to bottom — this is the same order used when recording markings on official documents.

Top of Face

Poll

The very top of the head, between the ears. Not a marking location, but the anatomical starting point when examining a horse's head.

Upper Face

Forehead

The broad, flat area between the eyes and below the forelock. The primary location for Stars and the top of Strips.

Mid Face

Face / Nasal Bone

The long, flat central section of the face running from below the eyes to above the nostrils. Strips and Blazes run along this region.

Lower Face

Muzzle

The soft, rounded area encompassing the nostrils, upper lip, and lower lip. The location for Snips and the lower extent of Blazes.

Soft Tissue

Nostrils

The paired breathing openings on the muzzle. Snips often appear between or on the edge of the nostrils.

Lower Soft Tissue

Lips & Chin

The upper and lower lips and chin area. Bald Faces and very large Blazes may extend into this region.

💡 Did You Know

No two horses have exactly the same combination of facial markings, which makes each horse uniquely recognizable.

Reading a Face from Top to Bottom

1

Start at the Forelock

Begin your examination at the forelock and poll. Note the colour of the skin and hair at the top of the forehead before any marking begins.

2

Assess the Forehead

Look at the flat area between the eyes. Any white patch here — even a faint smudge — is a Star and should be noted.

3

Follow the Nasal Bone

Trace your eye down the center of the face along the nasal bone. Any white strip running here is a Strip if it is thin, or it could be the middle portion of a blaze or bald face.

4

Examine the Muzzle

Look at the muzzle and nostrils carefully. White patches here are Snips. They can be small enough to fit between the nostrils, cover one nostril, or maybe blend into both.

5

Note the Full Extent

Record how far each marking extends — does the Strip reach the muzzle? Does the Blaze cover the nostrils? The full extent is part of the official description.

Three Main Areas

Forehead

The broad, flat area of the face between the eyes — the primary location for a Star marking.

Nasal Bone

The central bony ridge of the face running from the forehead to the muzzle — the region along which Strips and Blazes travel.

Muzzle

The soft lower portion of the face including the nostrils and lips — where Snips appear and where Blazes terminate.
