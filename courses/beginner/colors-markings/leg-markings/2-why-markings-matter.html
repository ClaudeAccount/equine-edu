Leg Markings

Why Leg Markings Matter

Leg markings help identify individual horses, but they need to be described clearly: which leg, how high the white reaches, and what details make the marking unique.

Used for Identification

White markings on the legs are commonly used for identification because they are easy to see, remain consistent throughout a horse's life, and help distinguish one horse from another. Some horses have white markings on one leg, several legs, or no leg markings at all.

Accurate descriptions include both the marking type and the specific leg it appears on. For example, “left hind sock” provides much clearer information than simply “sock,” since the same marking can occur on any of the horse’s four legs.

Why Location Matters

Leg markings are called different names depending on high the white goes on the leg. Depending on whether it covers the whole pastern, part of the pastern, or just one side of the coronet band will help to give the marking its name.

Which Leg?

Descriptions should name the exact limb: left front, right front, left hind, or right hind.

How High?

It is important to note how high the white travels up the leg. White found near the hoof may be a coronet or pastern depending on how high it goes up the leg under the fetlock. White that reaches above the fetlock may become a sock or stocking, depending on how high up the cannon bone it goes.

Complete or Partial?

Some markings wrap around the leg; others cover only part of the circumference. Partial markings should be described as partial rather than forced into a full marking label.

Extra Details

Ermine marks, uneven edges, dark spots, and irregular borders can make a marking more distinctive and should be included when accuracy matters.

How to Describe a Leg Marking

Use a consistent order when describing markings so they are clear and easy to read. Start with what side of the horse the marking is on, then whether it is a front or hind leg, followed by the type of marking, how high it goes, and any extra details.

Example: “Left hind sock with an uneven upper edge and small dark ermine marks near the coronet.”
