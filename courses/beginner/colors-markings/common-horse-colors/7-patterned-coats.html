Common Horse Colors

Patterned Coats

Pinto and Appaloosa are two of the most visually distinctive horse coat patterns - each produced by a completely different genetic mechanism, with its own unique visual characteristics.

Multi-Color Coat Patterns

Unlike the solid and semi-solid colors covered in earlier lessons, pinto and appaloosa patterns involve large areas of contrasting color or spotting distributed across the coat. These are patterns layered on top of a base color - not separate colors on their own.

Pinto refers to a pattern of large white patches over a colored coat. Appaloosa refers to a distinct family of spotting patterns associated with the Appaloosa breed and the leopard complex gene. Each pattern family has several subtypes - this lesson covers the most common ones a beginner will encounter.

Pinto is a coat pattern of large, irregular white patches over a base-colored coat. The two main pinto patterns are tobiano and overo - they look different and are produced by different genes. "Pinto" describes the pattern; the underlying body color can be any base color.

Pinto Pattern

Tobiano

White patches cross the topline (back and hindquarters) and the legs are often white. The head is usually the base color or has normal face markings. White patches tend to have smooth, rounded edges. Tobiano is the most common pinto pattern.

Pinto Pattern

Overo

White patches spread horizontally from the belly upward and rarely cross the topline. The legs are often darker. The face frequently shows bold white markings or a bald face. White patches have irregular, jagged edges and an overall "splashy" appearance.

Tobiano vs. Overo: The easiest rule: if white crosses the back (topline), it is likely tobiano. If white stays on the sides and belly without crossing the back, it is likely overo. When both patterns appear in one horse, it is called tovero.

Appaloosa spotting patterns are produced by the leopard complex (LP) gene, which creates a range of distinctive spotted coats. All Appaloosa patterns are associated with several identifying characteristics: mottled (speckled) skin around the muzzle and eyes, visible white sclera (the white part of the eye), and striped hooves.

Appaloosa Pattern

Leopard Appaloosa

A mostly white body coat covered in dark spots of various sizes - similar in appearance to a leopard's coat. Spots are usually dark and rounded. The most visually striking Appaloosa pattern.

Appaloosa Pattern

Blanket Appaloosa

A solid-colored body with a distinct white patch (blanket) over the hindquarters. The blanket may contain dark spots within it (spotted blanket) or be solid white (white blanket). The most common Appaloosa pattern.

Identifying Appaloosa Characteristics: Beyond the coat pattern, look for mottled skin (irregular dark and light patches) around the muzzle, lips, and eyes; a visible white sclera around the iris; and striped hooves. These traits appear in all Appaloosa horses regardless of coat pattern.
