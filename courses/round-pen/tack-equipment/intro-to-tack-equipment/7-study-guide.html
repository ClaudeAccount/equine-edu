Key Concepts

What is tack?

Any equipment placed on a horse to enable riding, driving, or safe handling — including the saddle, bridle, halter, and all related accessories.

Name the main parts of a saddle.

Pommel (front arch), seat, cantle (rear arch), skirt or flap, stirrup leather, stirrup, and cinch or girth. The tree is the internal frame that gives the saddle its shape and determines fit.

Name the main parts of a bridle.

Headstall (crown piece, cheek pieces, browband, throatlatch, noseband), bit, and reins.

What is a cinch vs. a girth?

A cinch holds a Western saddle in place; a girth holds an English saddle. Both encircle the barrel behind the elbow to secure the saddle.

What is a bit and what does it do?

A bit is placed in the horse's mouth and connects the bridle to the reins. It allows the rider to communicate direction and pace through rein pressure on the bars of the mouth.

Why does tack fit matter?

Poorly fitted tack causes pressure sores, back pain, and behavioral resistance. Correct fit distributes weight evenly and allows free movement.

What is the purpose of the browband?

The browband runs across the horse's forehead and prevents the crown piece of the bridle from sliding back off the poll.

What does the throatlatch do?

The throatlatch buckles under the jaw to keep the headstall from being pulled forward over the horse's ears.

Common Points of Confusion

Is a halter considered tack?

Yes — a halter is tack used for leading and tying. The broader category of tack includes all equipment worn by or placed on a horse for human use.

Does saddle type affect where the rider sits?

Yes. A Western saddle's deeper seat holds the rider more securely; an English saddle's flatter seat allows the leg contact needed for jumping and precision flatwork.
