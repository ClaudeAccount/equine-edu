The Arena

What are the two standard arena sizes?

The small standard arena is 20 meters wide by 40 meters long. The large standard arena is 20 meters wide by 60 meters long. All standard letter positions and figure sizes are based on these two dimensions.

Where are A and C located in a standard arena?

A is at the entry short end of the arena — the end where riders traditionally enter. C is at the opposite short end. Both letters mark the centerpoints of their respective short sides.

Where is X in a standard arena, and is it a physical letter on the wall?

X is the center point of the arena — located at the intersection of the center line (A to C) and the midline (E to B). It is not a physical letter on the wall — it is a reference point on the center line used to describe positions and figures.

What is the difference between the track, the quarter line, and the center line?

The track runs along the inside edge of the arena fence. The quarter line runs parallel to the long side, approximately 5 meters in from the rail. The center line runs from A to C through the exact middle of the arena.

What does "changing rein" mean?

Changing rein means changing direction — from tracking left (counterclockwise) to tracking right (clockwise) or vice versa. It is most commonly done across a diagonal, such as H to F.

Basic Figures

Where does a 20-meter circle at A touch in a 20x40 arena?

A 20-meter circle at A touches both long sides at K and F, the short end at A, and reaches X at its far point. The circle exactly fills the width of the arena and reaches halfway down its length.

What must happen at the center line when riding a serpentine?

The horse must change bend as it crosses the center line — left bend becomes right bend or vice versa. The rider prepares by straightening the horse briefly for one stride before applying the aids for the new bend.

Why is the center line considered a demanding straight track?

The center line runs through the middle of the arena with no wall on either side to assist with steering. Any drift or crookedness in the horse is immediately visible, making it the most revealing straight track in the arena.

School Movements

What footfall pattern does the rein-back use?

The rein-back steps backward in diagonal pairs — the same pairs as the trot footfall but in reverse. The movement should be straight, with no haunches drift, and willing rather than dragged.

What does the turn on the forehand teach the horse?

The turn on the forehand teaches the horse that leg behind the girth means "hindquarters, move away from this pressure." This response is the foundation for all lateral work — leg yield, shoulder-in, and more advanced movements.

How does a leg yield differ from a turn on the forehand?

A turn on the forehand is done from halt with the forehand nearly stationary and only the hindquarters moving sideways. A leg yield is performed in motion — the entire horse moves forward and sideways simultaneously, with the body straight rather than the hindquarters rotating around the forehand.
