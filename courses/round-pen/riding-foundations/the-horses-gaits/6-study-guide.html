Gait Names and Beat Counts

Name the four natural gaits of the horse in order from slowest to fastest.

Walk, trot, canter (lope), gallop. The walk is the slowest at roughly 4 mph; the gallop is the fastest, typically 25–30 mph and higher in racing.

How many beats does the walk have, and what is the footfall sequence?

The walk has four beats per stride cycle. The standard sequence is left hind, left fore, right hind, right fore (LH, LF, RH, RF). At least two hooves remain on the ground throughout — there is no moment of suspension.

How many beats does the trot have, and what moves together?

The trot has two beats per stride cycle. Diagonal leg pairs move together: left fore with right hind, then right fore with left hind. There is a brief moment of suspension between each beat.

How many beats does the canter have, and is there suspension?

The canter has three beats per stride cycle followed by a moment of suspension. On a right lead the sequence is left hind, then right hind and left fore together, then right fore (the leading leg), then suspension.

How does the gallop differ from the canter in footfall?

At the gallop, the diagonal pair that acts as a single beat in the canter splits into two separate beats, creating a four-beat pattern. The gallop also has a longer, more pronounced moment of suspension than the canter.

Leads and Terms

What is a lead at the canter, and why does it matter?

A lead identifies which foreleg reaches furthest forward in the canter stride. The correct lead matches the direction of travel — right lead when tracking or circling right, left lead when tracking or circling left. An incorrect lead creates imbalance through turns.

What is the difference between the canter and the lope?

The footfall pattern is identical. Lope is the Western term for the canter, ridden with less collection and impulsion, producing a slower, quieter stride suited to Western style and longer riding sessions.

What is the jog, and how does it relate to the trot?

The jog is the Western version of the trot. It uses the same two-beat diagonal footfall pattern but is performed at a slower, more relaxed pace with less impulsion than a working trot.

What is the difference between a simple lead change and a flying lead change?

In a simple lead change, the horse transitions back to the walk or trot before picking up the new lead. In a flying lead change, the horse switches leads mid-stride during the suspension phase without breaking gait.

Application and Observation

A horse is observed moving with all four hooves individual and a clear four-beat rhythm, never lifting all feet at once. Which gait is this?

The walk. It is the only natural gait with no suspension phase and produces a clear, even four-beat rhythm with each hoof landing separately.

A horse cantering in a right-hand circle has its left shoulder reaching further forward than its right. Is the horse on the correct lead?

No. On a right-hand circle, the right lead is correct — meaning the right foreleg should reach furthest forward. If the left shoulder is leading on a right circle, the horse is on the wrong lead (counter-canter).
