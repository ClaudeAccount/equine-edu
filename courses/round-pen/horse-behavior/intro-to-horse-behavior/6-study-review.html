Introduction to Horse Behavior

Study & Review

Vocabulary review, key principles, and Horse Bowl preparation for Introduction to Horse Behavior.

This module reviews the core vocabulary, concepts, and principles from Introduction to Horse Behavior. The material is organized for study, self-assessment, and preparation for Horse Bowl questions in the behavior and safety categories.

Core Vocabulary
Term

Prey animal

An animal whose primary survival strategy is detection and flight from predators, rather than combat.

Term

Flight response

The instinctive urge to flee from a perceived threat, hardwired in prey animals.

Term

Herd instinct

The drive to remain with conspecifics (members of the same species) as a collective safety strategy.

Term

Body language

Non-vocal communication through posture, ear position, eye expression, and movement.

More Vocabulary
Term

Arousal

The physiological state of heightened alertness and readiness for action, on a spectrum from calm to flight.

Term

Habituation

Learned reduction of response to a repeated, non-threatening stimulus.

Term

Nicker

A short, low, friendly vocalization directed at a known companion or person.

Term

Near side

The left side of the horse — the conventional side for approach, haltering, and mounting.

Horse Bowl Focus: Behavior questions frequently test (1) why horses are prey animals and what that means for handling, (2) reading specific body language signals, (3) safe handling protocols and why they exist, and (4) the definition of habituation. Be ready to explain why each safety rule exists, not just what it is.

Key Principles: (1) All horse behavior begins with prey animal biology — flight, herd, and heightened senses. (2) Body language signals must be read in combination, not individually. (3) Calm state is the prerequisite for learning — aroused horses do not retain training. (4) Every barn safety rule traces to a specific biological characteristic. (5) Anticipatory handling — adjusting before the horse reaches threshold — produces fewer incidents than reactive handling.
