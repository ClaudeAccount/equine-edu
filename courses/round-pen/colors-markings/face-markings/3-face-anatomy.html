Parts of the Face

Before you can identify facial markings, it helps to understand the basic areas of the horse's face where they occur. This makes it easier to understand how markings are described and named.

Face Marking Basics

Facial markings are named and described based on where they appear on the horse's face. A star is a white marking on the forehead, while a snip is a small white marking on or around the muzzle between the nostrils.

Learning the basic parts of the horse's face first makes it easier to understand how these markings are named and how to describe them clearly.

Parts of the Horse's Face

When recording facial markings, always work from the top of the horse's face downward. This follows the standard format used in official identification and documentation.

Top of Face

Poll

The very top of the head, between the ears. Not a marking location, but the anatomical starting point when examining a horse's head.

Upper Face

Forehead

The broad, flat area between the eyes and below the forelock. The primary location for stars and the upper portion of strips.

Mid Face

Face / Nasal Bone

The central section of the face running from below the eyes to above the nostrils. Strips and blazes commonly extend through this region.

Lower Face

Muzzle

The soft, rounded area that includes the nostrils and upper lip. The location of snips.

Soft Tissue

Nostrils

The paired openings used for breathing. Snips often appear between or along the edges of the nostrils.

Lower Soft Tissue

Lips & Chin

The lips and chin area of the horse's face. Bald faces and wide blazes may extend into this region.

💡 Did You Know

Facial markings often appear in similar types and combinations, but the exact pattern, placement, and shape vary from horse to horse, making each horse individually identifiable.

Reading a Face from Top to Bottom

1

Start at the Forelock

Begin your examination at the forelock and poll. Note the colour of the hair and skin at the top of the head before any facial markings begin.

2

Assess the Forehead

Examine the flat area between the eyes. Any white marking here — even a faint patch — is recorded as a star.

3

Follow the Nasal Bone

Move your focus down the center of the face along the nasal bone. A narrow white marking in this area is recorded as a strip. Wider markings that extend through this region may be part of a blaze or bald face.

4

Examine the Muzzle

Inspect the muzzle and nostrils carefully. White markings in this area are recorded as snips. These may appear between the nostrils, cover one nostril, or extend across both.

5

Note the Full Extent

Record the full extent of each marking. Note how far it extends and which areas it includes, as this is part of an accurate official description.

Three Main Areas

Forehead

The broad, flat area between the eyes on the face. This is the primary location for star markings.

Nasal Bone

The central ridge of the face running from the forehead to the muzzle. Strips and blazes follow this region.

Muzzle

The lower, soft portion of the face that includes the nostrils and lips. Snips appear here, and blazes may extend into this area.
