Color Identification

What is the first thing to check when identifying a horse's coat color?

Start with body color, then check mane and tail, then lower legs. These three together usually lock in the correct color term.

What is the key difference between roan and gray?

Both look like a mix of colored and white hairs, but roan stays stable throughout the horse's life and has a solid-colored head. Gray progressively lightens over time and eventually turns white all over, including the head.

Name the color families covered in this course.

Base coat colors (chestnut, black, bay), dilute colors (palomino, buckskin, cremello, dun), gray, roan, and patterned coats (pinto, Appaloosa).

What are black points, and which base coat color has them?

Black points are a black mane, tail, lower legs, and ear tips. Bay horses have black points. Chestnuts do not.

Common Mix-Ups

How do you tell a dark gray apart from a black horse?

Check the root color and any areas sheltered from sun (under the mane, inside the legs). True black has no warm or brown tones anywhere. Gray horses show white or mixed hairs at the roots and around the face, even if still very dark overall.

A horse has a reddish body with a flaxen (pale cream) mane and tail. What color is it?

Chestnut. The flaxen mane and tail is a variation of chestnut, not a separate color. The key is that the mane and tail are not black.

What separates a palomino from a chestnut?

Palomino is a dilute color: gold body with a white or cream mane and tail, produced by a dilute gene acting on a chestnut base. Chestnut has a red body with a matching or flaxen mane and tail, no gold tone.

Scenario Spot-Checks

A horse has a red-brown body, a black mane and tail, and black lower legs. What is its base color?

Bay. The defining feature is the black mane, tail, and legs (black points) on a red-brown body.

Someone describes a horse as ‘white.’ What is the most likely accurate color term, and what should be checked?

Most horses described as white are actually gray. Check the skin under the coat (gray horses have dark skin) and look for any remaining pigmented hairs. Truly white horses (white gene) are rare.
