Key Parts and Regions

Where is a horse's height measured, and what unit is used?

Height is always measured at the withers — the highest point of the back at the base of the neck. The unit is hands, where one hand equals four inches.

Name the three key topline landmarks from front to back.

Poll (top of the head), withers (base of the neck/top of the shoulder), and croup (top of the hindquarters).

Which leg parts appear on both front and hind legs below the knee or hock?

Cannon bone, fetlock, pastern, coronet band, and hoof appear on all four legs below the major joint (knee in front, hock behind).

What is the difference between the knee and the hock?

The knee is the large joint midway up the front leg. The hock is the large joint midway up the hind leg. Both are frequently discussed in soundness conversations.

Head and Body Parts

Name three parts of the horse's head.

Any three from: poll, forelock, ears, eye, face, nostril, muzzle, chin groove, jaw, cheek, throatlatch.

What is the barrel?

The barrel is the main body cavity of the horse — the rounded mid-section between the forelegs and the hindquarters.

What is the flank, and where is it located?

The flank is the area between the last rib and the hindquarters, on the side of the horse. It is visible when watching for respiratory rate.

Scenario Spot-Checks

A veterinarian says the horse has swelling at the fetlock. Which part of the leg is affected, and does this apply to a front leg, hind leg, or both?

The fetlock is the joint just above the pastern, below the cannon. It appears on all four legs, so the vet would specify which leg.

Someone says a horse measures 15.2 hands. What does that mean in practical terms?

Fifteen hands and two inches at the withers. One hand is four inches, so 15.2 hands is sixty-two inches, or roughly five feet two inches, measured at the withers.
