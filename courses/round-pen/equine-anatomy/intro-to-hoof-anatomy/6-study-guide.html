Outer and Underneath Structures

Which hoof structures are visible when the horse is standing?

The outer hoof wall, the coronet band, and the periople (a thin waxy layer at the top of the hoof wall) are visible from the outside when the horse is standing.

Which hoof structures are visible when the hoof is picked up and cleaned?

The sole, the frog, the white line, the bars, and the cleft of the frog become visible when the hoof is lifted and debris is removed.

What is the hoof wall's primary function?

The hoof wall is the outer hard structure that carries most of the horse's weight around the perimeter of the foot and protects the inner structures.

What is the frog, and what does it do?

The frog is a V-shaped rubbery structure on the underside of the hoof. It acts as a shock absorber, helps with traction, and assists with blood circulation in the lower leg when it makes contact with the ground.

Key Terms

What is the white line, and why does it matter?

The white line is the junction between the hoof wall and the sole, visible on the underside of the hoof. It is a common site for infections (white line disease) and is used as a guide for nail placement in shoeing.

What is the periople?

A thin, waxy layer at the very top of the hoof wall just below the coronet band. It helps seal moisture into the hoof wall.

What are the bars of the hoof?

The bars are extensions of the hoof wall that fold inward at the heels toward the frog. They provide additional support and help define the heel area.

Scenario Spot-Checks

A farrier asks you to pick up the right front hoof and check the underside. Which structures should be visible once it is cleaned out?

The sole, frog, white line, bars, and the cleft of the frog. These are all underneath structures visible only when the hoof is lifted and cleaned.

A horse has a crack running vertically down the hoof wall from the coronet band toward the ground. Which outer structure is affected?

The hoof wall. Quarter cracks and toe cracks are injuries to the hoof wall, the outer hard structure visible when the horse is standing.
