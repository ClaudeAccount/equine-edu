Course Curriculum

Each module builds on the last — from understanding why external observation matters, to studying every surface structure in depth, to reviewing and testing what you have learned.

Recommended: Complete Intro to Parts of the Horse before starting this course. That foundation course introduces the major regions and landmarks this course builds on.

Course Focus

What You Can See and Feel

This course focuses entirely on the exterior of the horse — the structures you can observe with your eyes and assess with your hands. For every part, you will learn what it looks like, how to evaluate it, what good conformation or condition looks like, and why it matters for daily horse care and horsemanship.

✓ Identify and assess every major external landmark from poll to hoof
✓ Evaluate conformation features like shoulder angle, pastern angle, and topline
✓ Use palpation to assess tendons, joints, and bony landmarks
✓ Recognize normal vs. concerning findings on a visual horse exam
✓ Understand what each external region tells you about soundness and health
✓ Communicate clearly about external structures with vets, farriers, and trainers

Develop Your Eye for Horses

Experienced horse people can walk up to a horse and immediately begin reading it — noticing musculature, joint filling, foot quality, topline, and more. That skill starts with knowing what to look for. This course gives you a systematic foundation for exactly that kind of observation.

  • Every region covered from a practical observation standpoint
  • Conformation assessment built into every section
  • Flip cards and quiz for active review and retention
  • Accurate, accessible content grounded in real horsemanship

Who This Course is For

  • Learners who have completed Intro to Parts of the Horse
  • Students preparing for Horse Bowl or similar knowledge competitions
  • Riders and handlers developing their eye for conformation and soundness
  • Anyone who wants to do a more informed visual and hands-on assessment of a horse
  • Instructors building student vocabulary and observation skills beyond basic landmarks
