Color Identification Scenarios

Read each horse description and select the correct base coat color.

Apply What You Know

Each scenario describes a real horse situation. Use the visual clues in the description — body color, mane, tail, leg color — to identify the base coat color. Select your answer to see instant feedback.

Scenario 1
A horse has a deep red-brown body and a black mane and tail. The lower legs are also black. What is the base coat color?
Correct! Black mane and tail over a red-brown body with black lower legs — those are the black points that confirm bay.
Not quite. Look for the black mane, tail, and lower legs on a red body. That combination is bay.
Scenario 2
A horse has a bright copper-red body with a mane and tail that are the same warm red color. No black anywhere. What is the base coat color?
Correct! Red body, matching red mane and tail, and no black points — that is a classic chestnut.
Not quite. A red body with a matching red mane and no black anywhere means chestnut.
Scenario 3
A horse appears almost black all over — body, mane, tail, and legs — with no visible red or brown tones anywhere. What is the most likely base coat color?
Correct! No warm tones, no contrast, uniform black across the whole horse — that is true black.
Not quite. Uniform black with no warm tones or contrast anywhere is true black.
Scenario 4
A horse has a very dark brown body that looks nearly black, but the mane is black and you can see faint brownish tones at the muzzle and flanks. What is the most likely base coat color?
Correct! Warm brown tones at the flanks and muzzle, combined with a black mane, indicate dark bay — not true black.
Not quite. Warm tones at the flanks and muzzle mean dark bay. True black has no warm tones anywhere.
Scenario 5
A horse has a very dark red-brown body and a mane that is the same dark brown-red. No black anywhere on the mane or lower legs. What is the base coat color?
Correct! This is a liver chestnut — a dark shade of chestnut. The matching dark mane and the absence of black points confirm it.
Not quite. A very dark body with a matching dark mane and no black points is liver chestnut — still a chestnut.
