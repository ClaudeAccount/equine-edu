Driving Harness & Equipment

Harness & Bridle Parts

Use the tabs to study the main parts of a driving harness, driving bridle, and vehicle connection system.

Parts of the Driving Harness

A driving harness connects the horse to the vehicle and helps manage pulling, holding back, steering, and balance. Exact harness styles vary, but the same core jobs show up again and again.

Breastcollar

The padded strap across the chest that the horse pushes into to pull many light vehicles.

Collar

A shaped padded collar used in some harnesses to spread pulling pressure around the shoulders.

Traces

The long straps or lines that connect the pulling part of the harness to the vehicle.

Saddle / Pad

The padded top piece that sits on the horse back and supports parts such as the terrets and shaft loops.

Girth / Bellyband

The strap under the belly that helps keep the harness saddle steady.

Breeching

The strap around the hindquarters that helps the horse slow or hold back the vehicle.

Crupper

The loop under the tail that helps keep the harness saddle from sliding forward.

Terrets

Rings on the harness saddle that guide the reins or lines back toward the driver.

Parts of the Driving Bridle

A driving bridle helps guide the horse from behind. Many driving bridles include blinders so the horse can focus forward and avoid seeing too much movement behind or beside them.

Crownpiece

The top strap that goes over the poll and supports the rest of the bridle.

Browband

The strap across the forehead that helps keep the crownpiece positioned.

Throatlatch

The strap under the throat area that helps prevent the bridle from slipping off.

Cheekpieces

The side straps that help hold the bit at the correct height.

Noseband

The strap around the nose that can help stabilize the bridle.

Blinders / Winkers

Side pieces that limit rear and side vision so the horse can stay focused forward.

Bit

The mouthpiece or attachment that connects to the reins or lines.

Lines / Reins

The long reins held by the driver to communicate from behind the horse.

Vehicle Connections

Driving equipment works as a system. The harness, bridle, vehicle, and driver all need to be connected correctly before the horse moves off.

Shafts

The long vehicle arms that run along either side of a single horse and help guide the vehicle.

Singletree / Whiffletree

A swinging bar that helps even out pull from the traces as the horse moves.

Holdback Straps

Straps that connect breeching to the shafts so the horse can help slow the vehicle.

Shaft Loops / Tugs

Loops that help support or position the shafts on the harness.

Neck Strap

A strap that can help support the breastcollar or keep front harness pieces positioned.

Safety Check

Before driving, check that leather, buckles, stitching, vehicle pins, reins, and attachments are secure and correctly adjusted.

Driving equipment can vary by vehicle, discipline, and harness style. The goal is to understand each part by its job: pulling, holding back, guiding, supporting, or stabilizing.
