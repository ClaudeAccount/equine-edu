Rider Safety Basics

Study Guide

A quick self-test. Try to answer each question before looking at the answer.

How to use this guide: try to answer each question before looking at the answer.

Key Concepts

What is the flight response?

The horse’s instinct to move away quickly from danger.

Where are a horse’s blind spots?

Directly behind the horse and directly in front of its face.

What makes a riding helmet correct for riding?

A riding helmet that is safety-tested for horseback riding and has the correct safety label inside.

What should riding boots be like?

Sturdy boots with a hard toe and a small heel.

Where do you lead a horse from?

At the horse’s left shoulder using a lead rope. Keep extra rope folded, not wrapped.

What knot is used when tying a horse?

A quick-release knot so the horse can be untied quickly if needed.

Common Mistakes

Pinned ears or relaxed ears — which is the warning?

Pinned ears flat against the neck can be a warning. Relaxed ears turned slightly back are not.

Where is the unsafe place to stand behind a horse?

A few feet directly behind the horse.

Why not use a bicycle helmet?

It is made for different sports, not riding horses.

What should a lead rope never be used for?

Never wrap it around your hand, wrist, or body. Never tie it to weak or movable objects.

Scenarios

A person is wearing sandals and wants to lead a horse. What must change first?

They need sturdy boots before handling a horse.

A horse is tied with reins to a gate. What is wrong?

Use a halter and lead rope, tie to something strong, and use a quick-release knot.

Someone walks straight toward a horse’s face. What should they do instead?

Walk toward the horse’s left shoulder at an angle and speak calmly.
