Gait Names and Beat Counts

Name the four natural gaits of the horse in order from slowest to fastest.

Walk, trot, canter (lope), gallop. The walk is the slowest, at about 4 mph. The gallop is the fastest, usually 25 to 30 mph, and even higher in racing.

How many beats does the walk have, and what is the footfall sequence?

The walk has four beats. The order is left hind, left fore, right hind, right fore (LH, LF, RH, RF). At least two hooves stay on the ground the whole time. There is no moment of suspension.

How many beats does the trot have, and what moves together?

The trot has two beats. The legs move in diagonal pairs: left fore with right hind, then right fore with left hind. There is a short moment of suspension between each beat.

How many beats does the canter have, and is there suspension?

The canter has three beats, followed by a moment of suspension. One common order is left hind, then right hind and left fore together, then right fore, then suspension.

How does the gallop differ from the canter in footfall?

At the gallop, the diagonal pair that makes one beat in the canter splits into two beats. This creates a four-beat pattern. The gallop also has a longer moment of suspension than the canter.

Gait Terms

What is the difference between the canter and the lope?

They are the same gait with the same footfall pattern. Lope is the Western name for the canter. The lope is usually slower and more relaxed, which suits Western riding and longer rides.

What is the jog, and how does it relate to the trot?

The jog is the Western version of the trot. It uses the same two-beat pattern, but it is slower and quieter than a working trot.

Application and Observation

A horse is moving with a clear four-beat rhythm, and all four feet never leave the ground at once. Which gait is this?

The walk. It has a clear, even four-beat rhythm, and each hoof lands on its own. It has no moment of suspension.

A horse is moving in diagonal pairs with a short moment when all four feet leave the ground. Which gait is this?

The trot. The legs move in diagonal pairs, and there is a short moment of suspension between each beat.
