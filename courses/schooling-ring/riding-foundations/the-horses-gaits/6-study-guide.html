Gait Names and Beat Counts

Name the four natural gaits of the horse in order from slowest to fastest.

The four natural gaits are walk, trot, canter (lope), and gallop. The walk is the slowest gait at approximately 4 miles per hour. The gallop is the fastest gait, typically 25–30 miles per hour in most horses, and higher in racehorses.

How many beats does the walk have, and what is the footfall sequence?

The walk is a four-beat gait. The footfall sequence is:

Left hind → Left fore → Right hind → Right fore (LH, LF, RH, RF)

At least two hooves remain on the ground at all times. There is no moment of suspension in the walk.

How many beats does the trot have, and what moves together?

The trot is a two-beat gait. The horse moves in diagonal pairs:

Left fore + Right hind → Right fore + Left hind

There is a brief moment of suspension between each diagonal pair when all four feet are off the ground.

How many beats does the canter have, and is there suspension?

The canter is a three-beat gait followed by a moment of suspension. One common sequence is:

Left hind → Right hind + Left fore (together) → Right fore → suspension

How does the gallop differ from the canter in footfall?

At the gallop, the diagonal pair seen in the canter separates into two distinct beats. This creates a four-beat gait. The gallop also includes a longer moment of suspension than the canter, allowing for maximum stride length and speed.

Gait Terms

What is the difference between the canter and the lope?

The canter and lope are the same gait with the same footfall pattern. “Lope” is the Western term for the canter. The lope is typically slower, lower, and more relaxed in presentation.

What is the jog, and how does it relate to the trot?

The jog is the Western version of the trot. It follows the same two-beat diagonal pattern but is slower, softer, and less forward in energy than a working trot.

Application and Observation

A horse is moving with a clear four-beat rhythm, and all four feet never leave the ground at once. Which gait is this?

This is the walk. It is a four-beat gait with a consistent rhythm and no suspension phase.

A horse is moving in diagonal pairs with a short moment when all four feet leave the ground. Which gait is this?

This is the trot. It is a two-beat diagonal gait with a brief suspension phase between each stride.
