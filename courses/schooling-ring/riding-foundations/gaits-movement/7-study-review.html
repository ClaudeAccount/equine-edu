Understanding Horse Movement

Study & Review

Vocabulary review, key principles, and Horse Bowl preparation for Gaits and Movement.

This module reviews core vocabulary and principles from Gaits and Movement for self-assessment and Horse Bowl preparation in the horsemanship, judging, and veterinary science categories.

Core Vocabulary A
Term

Impulsion

Energy generated from the hindquarters and directed forward into a controlled, elastic stride — distinct from speed.

Term

Suspension

The moment in the trot, canter, or gallop when all four feet are simultaneously off the ground.

Term

Counter-canter

Cantering on the outside lead intentionally, as a suppling and straightening gymnastic exercise.

Term

Half-halt

A brief, simultaneous application of seat, leg, and rein to collect the horse without producing a full transition.

Core Vocabulary B
Term

Overtracking

The hind foot landing in front of the print left by the same-side foreleg at the walk — an indicator of hindleg reach.

Term

Head bob

The characteristic up-down motion of a horse's head at the trot when lameness is present in a front limb.

Term

AAEP Lameness Scale

A 0–5 grading scale for equine lameness severity; 0 = sound, 5 = non-weight-bearing.

Term

Posting diagonal

The diagonal pair of legs the rider rises and sits with at the trot; correct diagonal = rise with outside shoulder.

Horse Bowl Focus: Gait questions target (1) footfall sequences and beat counts for all four gaits, (2) identification of the correct lead and diagonal, (3) impulsion vs. speed, (4) criteria used to evaluate movement quality, (5) the head bob and pelvic drop as lameness signs, and (6) the AAEP lameness scale grades.

Key Principles: (1) Walk = 4 beats, no suspension. Trot = 2 beats, diagonal pairs, suspension. Canter = 3 beats + suspension, lead-specific. Gallop = 4 beats, derived from canter. (2) Impulsion ≠ speed. (3) Correct lead: inside shoulder extends farther. Correct diagonal: rise with outside shoulder. (4) Movement evaluation order: rhythm → impulsion → straightness. (5) Head bob = front lameness. Pelvic drop = hind lameness. (6) AAEP 5 = emergency.
