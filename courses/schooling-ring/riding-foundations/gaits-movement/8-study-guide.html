Key Concepts

What are the three natural gaits of the horse?

Walk (4-beat), trot (2-beat diagonal), and canter/lope (3-beat with a moment of suspension). Gallop is a 4-beat gait distinct from the canter, and is the horse's fastest natural movement.

Describe the footfall sequence of the walk.

The walk is a 4-beat gait: left hind, left fore, right hind, right fore (or the reverse sequence). There is no moment of suspension — at least one foot is always on the ground.

Describe the footfall sequence of the trot.

The trot is a 2-beat diagonal gait. Diagonal pairs move together: left fore and right hind simultaneously, then right fore and left hind simultaneously, with a moment of suspension between each pair.

Describe the canter footfall sequence and identify the leading leg.

The canter is 3-beat: outside hind (1), inside hind and outside fore diagonal (2), inside fore/leading leg (3), followed by a moment of suspension. The leading leg is the last to leave the ground before suspension and appears to reach farthest forward.

What is a correct lead?

In the canter, the correct lead means the inside foreleg leads — the leg toward the inside of the circle or bend. On a circle to the left, the left foreleg is the leading leg.

What is a diagonal in the posting trot?

A diagonal is the pair of legs the rider rises with during the posting trot. On a circle, the rider should post on the outside diagonal — rising as the outside fore and inside hind hit the ground.

What is impulsion?

Impulsion is the energy generated from the hindquarters and directed through a supple back into forward movement. It is not speed — it is engagement of the hindquarters producing powerful, elastic strides.

What is the difference between suppleness and stiffness?

A supple horse moves with a soft, swinging back that allows energy to flow from hindquarters through the topline to the contact. A stiff horse blocks the energy at the back or poll, producing choppy, short strides.

Common Points of Confusion

Is the gallop the same as a fast canter?

No. The canter is a 3-beat gait with a moment of suspension. The gallop is a 4-beat gait where the diagonal pair of the canter splits — each leg lands independently. It is a distinct gait, not a faster version of the canter.

Does posting on the correct diagonal matter in a straight line?

In straight-line work, diagonal choice is less critical. Diagonal matters most on circles and curved lines to help the horse balance — posting on the outside diagonal (rising as outside fore and inside hind strike) encourages better balance through the turn.
