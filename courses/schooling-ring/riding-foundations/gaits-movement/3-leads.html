Leads, Diagonals & Canter Mechanics

Correct and incorrect leads, posting diagonals at the trot, and how to identify and correct each from the saddle and ground.

Mechanics First, Rules Second

The canter lead and the posting diagonal are among the most frequently evaluated criteria in horsemanship classes and among the most frequently confused concepts for developing riders. Understanding each begins with the mechanics — what is happening under the saddle at the moment a lead or diagonal is determined — rather than simply a memorized rule for checking afterward.

A horse on the correct lead and a rider on the correct diagonal are both optimizing the biomechanical balance of the movement in the direction of travel. These are not arbitrary conventions — they reflect the mechanical structure of the gait itself.

💡 Did You Know

Counter-canter — cantering on the outside lead intentionally — is not a mistake. It is a suppling and straightening exercise used in advanced schooling work. A horse working correctly in counter-canter is performing a gymnastic movement requiring more collection and body control than a simple working canter. This distinction matters in judging contexts where counter-canter patterns may be included in a class.

Leads and Diagonals
Concept

The Canter Lead

In the canter, the footfall sequence determines the lead. On the left lead, the right hind strikes first, then the left hind and right fore together as the diagonal pair, then the left fore — the leading leg — lands last. The leading foreleg extends farther forward and is the last to leave the ground before the moment of suspension. A horse cantering in a left circle should be on the left lead; the leading leg provides balance and support through the arc of the turn.

Concept

Identifying the Lead

From the saddle: feel whether the inside hip is carried slightly higher than the outside hip — on the correct lead, the inside hip follows the extended motion of the leading leg. Visually from the saddle: glance down at the shoulder — the leading shoulder swings farther forward on the correct lead. From the ground: the leading foreleg is clearly visible as the leg that extends farther forward with each stride.

Concept

The Wrong Lead

A horse on the wrong lead will feel unbalanced through turns and may tend to fall in or drift out. The correction is to bring the horse back to trot, rebalance with a half-halt, and ask for the canter again with a clear inside leg aid. Correct lead departures require the horse to be balanced and slightly flexed to the inside before the aid is applied — rushing the departure without preparation increases the chance of a wrong lead departure.

Concept

The Posting Diagonal

At the trot, the rider posts in rhythm with the diagonal pairs. On the correct diagonal, the rider rises when the outside foreleg and inside hindleg (the outside diagonal pair) move forward together. This positions the rider's weight optimally for the horse's balance through a turn. The rule: rise with the outside shoulder. To check: glance at the inside shoulder — if it moves forward as the rider sits, the diagonal is incorrect.

The visual lead check: glance at the inside shoulder during the canter. If the inside shoulder swings farther forward than the outside shoulder, the horse is on the correct lead. This check can be completed within the first two strides of a canter departure and should become an automatic habit.

Counter-canter — intentionally cantering on the outside lead — is a gymnastic suppling and straightening exercise used in schooling. A horse working correctly in counter-canter is not on a wrong lead; it is performing a movement that requires more collection and body control than working canter. A horse that falls into counter-canter unasked has taken the wrong lead; a horse asked for counter-canter in a pattern is demonstrating trained balance. This distinction matters in horse judging and horsemanship class evaluation.

Things to Remember

  • Left lead canter: right hind → left hind + right fore → left fore (leading leg, which extends farthest forward).
  • Identify lead: glance at the inside shoulder — it swings farther forward on the correct lead.
  • Posting diagonal: rise with the outside shoulder (inside shoulder moves forward as the rider sits).
  • Counter-canter is intentional — distinguish it from an accidental wrong lead in judging and schooling contexts.
