Leads, Diagonals & Canter Mechanics Canter Mechanics

Correct and incorrect leads, posting diagonals at the trot, and how to identify and correct each from the saddle and ground.

The canter lead and the posting diagonal are two of the most frequently tested concepts in horsemanship and one of the most frequently confused by beginning riders. Understanding each begins with understanding the mechanics that produce it — what is happening under the saddle at the moment the rider determines a lead or diagonal — not just a set of rules for checking.

A horse on the correct lead and a rider on the correct diagonal are both optimizing the biomechanical efficiency of movement. These are not arbitrary conventions; they reflect the rotational symmetry of the gait mechanics described in the previous module.

Leads and Diagonals
Concept 1

The Canter Lead

In the canter, the footfall sequence determines the lead. On the left lead, the right hind strikes first, then the left hind and right fore together, then the left fore — the leading leg — lands last. The leading foreleg extends farther and lands in front of the other. A horse cantering in a left circle should be on the left lead; the leading foreleg is on the inside of the circle, providing balance and facilitating turning.

Concept 2

Identifying the Lead

From the saddle: feel whether the inside hip is carried slightly higher than the outside hip — on the correct lead, the inside hip follows the leading leg's extended motion. Visually: glance down at the shoulder — the leading shoulder (inside) swings farther forward on the correct lead. From the ground: the leading foreleg is clearly visible as the leg that extends farther on each stride.

Concept 3

The Wrong Lead

A horse cantering on the incorrect or 'wrong' lead (counter-canter when done intentionally; disunited when the canter is irregular) will feel unbalanced in turns and may land cross-cantering if the diagonal pair is separated. The correction is to bring the horse back to trot, rebalance, and ask for the canter again with a clear inside leg aid. Correct lead departures require the horse to be balanced and slightly flexed to the inside before the canter aid is applied.

Concept 4

The Posting Diagonal

At the trot, the rider posts (rises and sits) in rhythm with the trot's diagonal pairs. On the correct diagonal, the rider rises when the inside foreleg and outside hindleg (the outside diagonal pair) move forward. This matches the rider's weight to the diagonal that has the most support during a turn. The rule: rise with the outside shoulder. Check by glancing at the inside shoulder — if the rider is sitting when the inside shoulder goes forward, the diagonal is incorrect; stand, sit, and recheck.

The test for a correct lead used in competitive showmanship is: glance at the inside shoulder during the canter. If the inside shoulder visibly swings forward more than the outside shoulder, the horse is on the correct lead. This visual check can be done within the first two strides and should become an automatic habit whenever a canter departure is made.

Counter-canter — intentionally cantering on the outside lead — is a suppling and straightening exercise used in schooling. It requires the horse to maintain balance and the correct three-beat sequence while bending away from the lead. A horse working correctly in counter-canter is not on a wrong lead; it is performing a gymnastic exercise that requires more collection and body control than working canter. This distinction matters in judging contexts where counter-canter may be included in a pattern.

Things to Remember

  • Left lead canter: right hind → left hind + right fore → left fore (leading leg extends farthest)
  • Identify lead: glance at inside shoulder — it swings farther forward on the correct lead
  • Correct diagonal rule: rise with the outside shoulder (inside shoulder comes forward as rider sits)
  • Counter-canter is intentional — distinguish it from a wrong lead in judging and schooling contexts
