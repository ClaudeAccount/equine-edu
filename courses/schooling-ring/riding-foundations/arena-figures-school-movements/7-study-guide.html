The Arena

What are the two standard arena sizes?

The small standard arena is 20 meters by 40 meters. The large standard arena is 20 meters by 60 meters. These dimensions are used in dressage and determine standard letter placement and figure sizing.

Where are A and C located in a standard arena?

A is at one short end of the arena (the entry end in traditional setup). C is at the opposite short end. Both mark the center points of their respective short sides.

Where is X in a standard arena, and is it a physical letter on the wall?

X is the center point of the arena. It sits on the intersection of the center line (A to C) and the line between E and B.

X is not marked on the arena wall. It is an imaginary reference point used for figures and movements.

What is the difference between the track, the quarter line, and the center line?
  • Track: The path along the inside edge of the arena fence
  • Quarter line: A line parallel to the long side, about 5 meters in from the rail
  • Center line: A straight line from A to C through the middle of the arena
What does "changing rein" mean?

Changing rein means changing direction — from tracking left (counterclockwise) to tracking right (clockwise), or the reverse. It is commonly done across diagonals to help balance the horse on both sides.

Arena Figures

What is the difference between riding the full arena and the half arena?

The full arena uses the entire space around the track. The half arena uses only one half of the arena, divided across the center line. This is often used for shared space or more focused work.

What is a volte?

A volte is a small circle, usually about 6 meters in diameter. It requires more bend, balance, and engagement than a larger circle.

What is a figure eight?

A figure eight is two equal circles joined together in the center, forming the shape of an “8.” The horse changes bend in the middle, smoothly switching direction.

What happens at the center line when riding a serpentine?

A serpentine is a series of connected loops across the arena in an S shape. Each time the horse crosses the center line, it briefly straightens before bending the other direction.

How is a simple serpentine different from a full serpentine?

A simple serpentine uses smaller, shallower loops, usually along one long side of the arena. A full serpentine uses larger loops that cross the entire arena.

Changes of Rein

What does "change of rein" mean?

A change of rein means switching direction — from tracking left to tracking right, or vice versa. It is used to keep the horse working evenly on both sides.

What is a change across the whole arena, and how is the half-arena change different?

A full change of rein uses a long diagonal across the arena, passing through X. A half change uses a shorter diagonal that only crosses part of the arena, often ending or starting on the center line.

What is the difference between a change from the circle and a change through the circle?

A change from the circle leaves the circle and continues in the opposite direction on a new line. A change through the circle stays within curved lines and uses two half-circles in an S shape to switch direction smoothly.
