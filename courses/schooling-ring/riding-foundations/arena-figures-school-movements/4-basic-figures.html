Introduction to Arena Figures

Arena Figures

Arena figures are planned shapes and paths ridden in the arena. They give riding structure by asking the horse to move in straight lines, curves, and transitions between bend. This lesson introduces the most common figures and what each one is meant to develop in the horse.

The Shapes Riders Follow

Arena figures are standard patterns used across English, Western, and driving disciplines. Because the names stay consistent, riders, trainers, and judges can all picture the same movement, no matter where they are.

This lesson is a simple introduction to the most common figures and their purpose in training.

💡 Did You Know

On circles, riders are taught to look ahead along the curve instead of down at the horse. The eyes help guide the body, and the body helps guide the horse. This is one of the first skills used to keep circles round and accurate.

Some figures are based on how the rider uses the arena itself.

Using the Arena Space

Full Arena. Riding the full arena means using the entire space along the track. This allows for large movements and full patterns.

Half Arena. Riding the half arena means working in one half of the space, divided across the center line. This is often used when multiple riders share the arena or when focusing on a smaller area.

Riding the Length. Riding the length means traveling straight from one end of the arena to the other along the long side. Even though it looks simple, maintaining a straight line requires balanced aids and consistent rhythm.

Key Idea: Arena work is built on space awareness — full arena, half arena, and straight lines all create the foundation for figures.

Circles are one of the most important arena figures. They build balance, rhythm, and correct bend.

Circles, Voltes, and Figure Eights

Circle. A circle is a round, even figure where the horse follows the same curve throughout. Common sizes include:

  • 20-meter circle: used for training and basic work
  • 10-meter circle: smaller and more advanced, requiring more balance and bend

Volte. A volte is a small circle, usually about 6 meters. It requires greater bend, engagement, and control because of its tight size.

Figure Eight. A figure eight is two equal circles joined together. The horse changes bend in the center, smoothly switching from one direction to the other.

Key Idea: Circles develop steady bend. Voltes increase difficulty. Figure eights teach smooth changes of bend and direction.

Serpentines are flowing figures that move across the arena in a series of loops. They are used to practice rhythm, bend, and smooth direction changes.

Serpentines and Simple Serpentines

Serpentine. A serpentine is made of equal loops that cross the arena. Each time the horse crosses the center line, it straightens briefly before bending the other way.

Simple Serpentine. A simple serpentine uses smaller, shallower loops, usually along a long side. It is often used as an introduction before riding full serpentines across the arena.

Key Idea: Serpentines are connected loops that require steady rhythm and clean changes of bend.
Barn Connection: Arena figures are easier to ride when they are planned ahead. Looking where the rider wants to go helps the horse stay balanced and follow the intended shape. Without a clear picture, figures tend to become uneven or drift off course.

Things to Remember

  • Arena figures are planned shapes that guide movement and training.
  • The arena can be used fully, halfway, or along straight lines.
  • Circles build balance and bend.
  • Voltes are small circles that require more control.
  • Figure eights teach changes of bend and direction.
  • Serpentines are linked loops that cross the arena with smooth transitions.
