Introduction to Arena Figures

Changes of Rein

A change of rein means changing the direction the horse travels around the arena. There are a few simple ways to do it — straight across the arena, or by using a circle.

Changing Direction

In the arena, "tracking left" means going one way (counterclockwise) and "tracking right" means going the other way (clockwise). A change of rein simply means switching from one direction to the other.

Riders change the rein often so the horse works evenly on both sides. This lesson gives a light look at four common ways to change the rein.

💡 Did You Know

Working a horse evenly in both directions matters. Like people, horses are often a little stronger or more flexible on one side. Changing the rein often helps both sides develop in a balanced way.

One simple way to change direction is to ride straight across the arena on a slanted line called a diagonal.

Changing Across the Arena

Change across the whole arena. The horse rides a long, slanted line all the way across the arena, from one corner area to the opposite one, passing through the center point (X). This crosses the whole space and turns the horse onto the new direction.

Change across the half arena. This uses a shorter slanted line that crosses only half of the arena, such as from a long-side letter to the center line. It changes direction over a shorter distance than the full diagonal.

Key Idea: A change across the whole arena uses a long diagonal through the center (X). A change across the half arena uses a shorter diagonal that crosses only half the space.

A rider can also change direction using a circle, without crossing the whole arena.

Changing on a Circle

Change from the circle. The horse is on a circle, then leaves the circle on a new line going the other way. The circle flows smoothly into the change of direction.

Change through the circle. The horse changes direction inside the circle by riding two smaller half-circles that form an S shape. The bend changes in the middle, and the horse ends up going the opposite way around.

Key Idea: A change from the circle leaves the circle onto a new line. A change through the circle uses two half-circles (an S shape) to turn the horse the other way.
Barn Connection: Changing the rein is not just for tests. Working both directions in everyday riding helps keep the horse balanced, supple, and comfortable.

Things to Remember

  • A change of rein means switching the direction the horse travels (tracking left or right).
  • A change across the whole arena uses a long diagonal through the center (X).
  • A change across the half arena uses a shorter diagonal across half the space.
  • A change from the circle leaves the circle onto a new line going the other way.
  • A change through the circle uses two half-circles in an S shape to switch direction.
  • Changing the rein often keeps the horse working evenly on both sides.
