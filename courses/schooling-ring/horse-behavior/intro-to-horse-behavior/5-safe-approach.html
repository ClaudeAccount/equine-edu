Introduction to Horse Behavior

Safe Approach & Handling

How to approach, halter, lead, tie, and release a horse. These steps help keep both the horse and handler safe.

Safe handling includes how to approach, halter, lead, tie, and release a horse. These steps help keep both the horse and handler safe.

Safe Handling Principles
Step 1

The Approach

Always approach a horse from the front-left side, near the shoulder.

  • Do not approach from directly in front
  • Do not approach from directly behind
  • Speak before touching the horse
  • Move slowly and predictably

This helps the horse see and hear you before contact.

Step 2

Haltering and Leading

Before putting on a halter:

  • Place the lead rope over the horse’s neck first
  • Then put on the halter

This helps prevent the horse from walking away during haltering.

When leading:

  • Walk at the horse’s shoulder
  • Do not walk in front of the horse
  • Do not walk behind the horse

If the horse moves into your space, calmly step it back and re-establish position.

Step 3

Tying

When tying a horse:

  • Tie only to strong, fixed structures
  • Do not tie to gates or loose fencing
  • Tie at or above the height of the horse’s withers
  • Always use a quick-release knot

If a horse pulls back while tied, give it space and wait until it settles before approaching.

Step 4

Releasing

When turning a horse out:

  • Walk the horse into the space
  • Turn the horse to face the gate
  • Step back before removing the halter

This helps prevent sudden spinning or kicking when the horse is released.

💡 Did You Know

A quick-release knot is designed so it can be undone quickly, even under tension. This makes it safer than a standard knot when working with horses.

In the Barn

Safe handling becomes easier with practice. Experienced handlers follow these steps automatically.

Speaking to a horse before touching it, approaching from the shoulder, and staying out of unsafe positions are basic habits that help keep horses calm and predictable.

Things to Remember

  • Always approach from the front-left side near the shoulder
  • Speak before touching the horse
  • Lead at the horse’s shoulder, not in front or behind
  • Tie only to solid structures using a quick-release knot
  • Turn the horse to face the gate before releasing it
