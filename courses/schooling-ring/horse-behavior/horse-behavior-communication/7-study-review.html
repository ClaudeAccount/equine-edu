Horse Behavior & Communication

Study & Review Review

Vocabulary review, key principles, and Horse Bowl preparation for Horse Behavior and Communication.

This module reviews the core vocabulary and principles from Horse Behavior and Communication. Content is organized for self-assessment and Horse Bowl preparation in the behavior, training, and animal welfare categories.

Core Vocabulary A
Term

Ethology

The scientific study of animal behavior in natural conditions.

Term

Negative reinforcement

The removal of an aversive stimulus following a desired behavior — the primary mechanism in pressure-and-release training.

Term

Stereotypy

A repetitive, invariant behavior that serves no apparent function; an indicator of chronic stress or welfare compromise.

Term

Systematic desensitization

Gradual exposure to a feared stimulus at sub-threshold intensity, allowing extinction of the fear response.

Core Vocabulary B
Term

Classical conditioning

Learning through association of a neutral stimulus with a meaningful one.

Term

Release timing

The moment at which pressure is removed following a desired behavioral response — the most important variable in negative reinforcement training.

Term

Horse Grimace Scale

A validated pain assessment tool that evaluates facial tension in horses across specific anatomical action units.

Term

Five Domains

A welfare assessment framework evaluating nutrition, physical environment, health, behavioral interactions, and mental state.

Horse Bowl Focus: Behavior questions target (1) ethology definitions, (2) the five natural behaviors, (3) the mechanics of negative vs. positive reinforcement, (4) release timing, (5) stereotypy causes and management, and (6) behavioral signs of pain. Know these topics with precision.

Key Principles: (1) Ethology reframes behavior as a predictable response to a trigger, not a personality problem. (2) Release timing — not pressure — is what horses learn from. (3) Stereotypies signal welfare compromise; blocking the behavior does not address the cause. (4) Systematic desensitization is evidence-superior to flooding. (5) Behavioral welfare assessment should be part of every management routine.
