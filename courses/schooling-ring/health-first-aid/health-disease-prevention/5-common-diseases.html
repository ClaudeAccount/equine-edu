Equine Health & Disease Prevention

Common Equine Diseases Diseases

Recognition, transmission, and prevention of the diseases most commonly encountered in domestic horse management.

Recognizing common equine diseases — their signs, causes, transmission routes, and appropriate initial responses — is a core competency for anyone responsible for horse care. Early recognition and prompt veterinary contact can make the difference between uncomplicated recovery and permanent damage or death in several common equine conditions.

This module covers both infectious diseases (caused by pathogens and capable of spreading between horses) and non-infectious conditions (caused by management, nutrition, or environment). Both categories require systematic understanding for effective prevention and appropriate response.

Common Equine Diseases

Strangles (Streptococcus equi)

Strangles is a highly contagious upper respiratory infection caused by Streptococcus equi subsp. equi. Signs include fever, nasal discharge, and swelling and abscessation of the lymph nodes under the jaw and in the throatlatch area. Spread occurs through direct contact and through contaminated equipment, water sources, and fomites. Biosecurity protocol during an outbreak: strict isolation, dedicated equipment, no horses leaving or entering the property until two clear culture samples confirm resolution.

Equine Influenza

Equine influenza is a highly contagious viral respiratory disease spread by aerosolized droplets from coughing horses. Signs include fever (up to 106°F), dry harsh cough, and nasal discharge. The virus spreads rapidly through groups of horses at shows, sales, and training facilities. Rest is the most important treatment — horses returned to work before full recovery risk developing bacterial bronchopneumonia as a secondary complication.

Equine Herpesvirus (EHV)

EHV-1 and EHV-4 cause respiratory disease; EHV-1 also causes abortion in mares, neurological disease (equine herpesvirus myeloencephalopathy, EHM), and neonatal illness. EHM outbreaks at horse shows and events have occurred with sufficient frequency to result in mandatory reporting requirements in most states. Fever is the first sign — serial temperature monitoring detects cases early, before neurological signs develop.

Founder / Laminitis

Laminitis — inflammation and compromise of the laminar connection between the coffin bone and hoof wall — is classified as a disease when it results from systemic illness (sepsis, retained placenta) rather than from carbohydrate overload. Regardless of cause, the mechanical consequence is the same: potential coffin bone rotation or sinking within the hoof capsule. Early recognition and immediate veterinary contact are critical.

Fever is the earliest and most consistent sign of many infectious equine diseases. A rectal temperature above 101.5°F in a resting adult horse is abnormal. Taking daily rectal temperatures during disease outbreaks or following high-exposure events (shows, new horse introductions) enables early detection before clinical signs are obvious — when isolation is most effective and treatment is most likely to succeed.

Non-infectious conditions commonly encountered in horse management include heaves (equine asthma), Cushing's disease (PPID), and metabolic syndrome (EMS). These conditions are not transmitted between horses but significantly affect management decisions around feed, exercise, and medication. Cushing's disease in particular becomes increasingly common as horses age past 15–20 years and is associated with a distinctive coat that fails to shed normally, plus increased drinking, urination, and susceptibility to infection.

Things to Remember

  • Temperature >101.5°F at rest = abnormal; daily monitoring during outbreaks enables early isolation
  • Strangles: highly contagious bacterial; strict isolation + no horse movement until two clear cultures
  • EHM (neurological herpesvirus): mandatory reporting in most states; fever monitoring is key
  • Cushing's disease (PPID): very common in horses >15–20 years; long wavy coat that fails to shed is hallmark
