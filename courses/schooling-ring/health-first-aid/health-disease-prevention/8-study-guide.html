Key Concepts

What are equine vital signs and what are their normal ranges?

Temperature: 99–101°F (37.2–38.3°C). Pulse (heart rate): 28–44 beats per minute. Respiration: 12–20 breaths per minute. Gut sounds: present in all four quadrants. Capillary refill time: under 2 seconds (pink gums).

What is the 'core' vaccination group for horses in North America?

Tetanus, Eastern and Western Equine Encephalomyelitis (EEE/WEE), West Nile Virus, and Rabies. These are recommended annually for all horses regardless of risk level by the American Association of Equine Practitioners (AAEP).

What is the purpose of an annual Coggins test?

The Coggins test detects antibodies to Equine Infectious Anemia (EIA), a reportable viral disease with no vaccine or treatment. Many states and events require a negative test for entry or transport.

What is an anthelmintic and how is resistance managed?

An anthelmintic (dewormer) kills internal parasites. Resistance is managed through fecal egg counts to identify actual parasite loads — treating only horses with significant burdens — rather than deworming all horses on a fixed calendar schedule.

What are the signs of colic?

Pawing, looking at the flank, rolling, reluctance to move, elevated heart rate, lack of gut sounds, sweating, and depressed attitude. Colic is abdominal pain and can range from mild to life-threatening.

What is a dental float and how often is it recommended?

Floating is the process of filing down sharp points and hooks on a horse's teeth that form as it grinds feed. It is typically recommended annually — a veterinarian or equine dentist performs it using hand or power floats.

What are the signs of a healthy horse at daily observation?

Bright and alert demeanor, eating and drinking normally, normal manure production, no swelling or heat in legs, no nasal discharge, normal respiratory rate, and standing comfortably without favoring limbs.

What does 'biosecurity' mean in an equine facility?

Practices that prevent the introduction and spread of infectious disease. Core practices include isolating new arrivals, keeping vaccination records current, maintaining clean water sources, and limiting contact with horses of unknown health status.

Common Points of Confusion

Should all horses be dewormed on the same schedule?

No. Research has shown that a small percentage of horses carry the majority of the parasite burden. Fecal egg counts identify which horses actually need treatment. Blanket calendar deworming drives anthelmintic resistance.

Is a horse that is eating and moving normally healthy?

Not necessarily. Some conditions — early laminitis, dental pain, low-grade colic, respiratory infections — can exist without obvious behavioral changes early on. Daily hands-on observation (checking legs for heat, gums for color, gut for sounds) catches problems the eye alone misses.
