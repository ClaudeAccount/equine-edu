Equine Health & Disease Prevention

Study & Review Review

Vocabulary review, key principles, and Horse Bowl preparation for Equine Health and Disease Prevention.

This module reviews core vocabulary and principles from Equine Health and Disease Prevention for self-assessment and Horse Bowl preparation in the veterinary science and health management categories.

Core Vocabulary A
Term

Core vaccine

A vaccine recommended for all horses regardless of geographic location or use, addressing diseases with high severity or broad geographic distribution.

Term

Fecal egg count (FEC)

A laboratory test measuring parasite eggs per gram of feces, used to classify horses as low, moderate, or high shedders.

Term

Anthelmintic resistance

The ability of a parasite population to survive drug treatment; a growing problem with small strongyles.

Term

Hypsodont

Having high-crowned teeth that erupt continuously; the dental type of horses, requiring regular professional floating.

Core Vocabulary B
Term

Strangles

Highly contagious bacterial upper respiratory disease caused by Streptococcus equi; characterized by lymph node abscesses.

Term

Quidding

Dropping partially chewed feed from the mouth; a primary behavioral indicator of dental pain.

Term

EHM

Equine Herpesvirus Myeloencephalopathy; the neurological form of EHV-1 infection, a mandatory reportable condition in most states.

Term

PPID (Cushing's disease)

Pituitary Pars Intermedia Dysfunction; a common endocrine disease of senior horses, causing a thick wavy coat, increased urination and drinking, and reduced immune function.

Horse Bowl Focus: Health questions target (1) core vaccine diseases and their vectors, (2) FEC-based deworming strategy, (3) disease recognition signs, (4) biosecurity protocols for outbreaks, (5) dental care frequency and indicators of dental problems, and (6) signs of PPID in senior horses.

Key Principles: (1) Core vaccines protect against EEE, WEE, West Nile, Tetanus, and Rabies — all annual. (2) FEC, not calendar rotation, drives deworming decisions. (3) Daily temperature monitoring during outbreaks enables early detection. (4) Annual dental exam for all horses over age 3. (5) Quidding and weight loss are the most common signs of advanced dental disease. (6) PPID is the most common endocrine disease in horses over age 15.
