Equine First Aid Basics

Recognizing Emergencies

Knowing the difference between a problem that can wait and one that needs an immediate call is one of the most important first aid skills.

What Counts as a True Emergency

Not every horse problem needs an emergency vet call. Some things can wait for a normal appointment. Others cannot wait because the horse's condition can get worse quickly.

A true emergency is any situation where waiting could cause more pain, more damage, or danger to the horse's life. The most common emergencies include colic, serious wounds, sudden severe lameness, breathing problems, eye injuries, and nervous system (brain or nerve) issues. Knowing the difference between "can wait" and "call now" is one of the most important first aid skills in horse care.

💡 Did You Know

A resting horse's heart rate is usually 28 to 44 beats per minute. During colic or serious pain, it can rise above 60. A high heart rate is one of the clearest early warning signs that a horse is in trouble.

Vital Signs

The Normal Ranges (What "Healthy" Looks Like)

Vital signs help show how a horse is doing inside the body. These are normal ranges for an adult horse at rest:

Vital Sign

Heart Rate (Pulse)

28 to 44 beats per minute. Higher than 60 at rest can mean strong pain or an emergency.

Vital Sign

Breathing Rate

8 to 16 breaths per minute. Fast or difficult breathing at rest can be serious.

Vital Sign

Temperature

99.5°F to 101.5°F. Above 102°F is a fever. Above 104°F is an emergency.

Vital Sign

Capillary Refill Time (Gum Check)

1 to 2 seconds. Press the gums, then release. Color should return quickly. Slow return can mean poor circulation.

Knowing these normal numbers helps a handler notice when something is wrong.

When in doubt, call the veterinarian. Waiting to see if a problem goes away is fine for small cuts and scrapes. It is not safe for colic signs, serious wounds, severe lameness, or any vital sign clearly outside the normal range.

Conditions Requiring Immediate Veterinary Contact

Conditions That Require Immediate Vet Care

These problems need an immediate call to a veterinarian. Waiting can make them worse or put the horse's life at risk.

Emergency 1

Colic Signs

Colic is stomach or intestinal pain. It is the most common horse emergency. Signs include pawing the ground, looking at or biting the belly, rolling or trying to roll, sweating without work, not eating, and a fast heart rate. Even mild colic signs should be taken seriously because they can get worse fast.

Emergency 2

Serious Wounds

Call a vet right away if a wound is near a joint, is deep or gaping open, is bleeding heavily or pulsing, is a puncture (especially in the hoof), or involves the eye or eyelid. Small surface cuts may not be emergencies, but deep or risky wounds are.

Emergency 3

Sudden Severe Lameness

A horse that suddenly cannot put weight on a leg or is extremely lame needs immediate attention. This can mean a broken bone, a severe injury, a hoof problem, or a laminitis episode. The horse should not be forced to move.

Emergency 4

Breathing, Eye, or Nervous System Signs

Call a vet immediately if any of these appear. Choke (a blockage in the throat): feed or saliva coming from the nose, coughing, or stretching the neck. Eye injury: squinting, swelling, cloudiness, or holding the eye shut. Nervous system signs: stumbling, head tilt, falling, shaking, or acting confused.

In the Barn

Before calling the vet, it helps to collect simple facts: heart rate, breathing rate, temperature, gum refill time, and what the horse is doing. Clear numbers help the vet understand how serious the situation is and how quickly help is needed.

Things to Remember

  • Normal vital signs help show when a horse is healthy.
  • Heart rate above 60 at rest is a serious warning sign.
  • Colic signs always need fast attention.
  • Deep wounds, eye injuries, and sudden severe lameness are emergencies.
  • When unsure, calling the vet is always the safest choice.
