Key Concepts

What is the handler's role in an equine first aid situation?

The handler's role is to keep the situation stable until the veterinarian arrives, not to replace the vet. This means keeping the horse calm and safe, checking and writing down vital signs, keeping the problem from getting worse, and giving the vet clear information when you call for help.

What are the four elements of equine first aid readiness?

A stocked first aid kit, knowing normal vital signs, being able to recognize emergencies, and having the veterinarian's contact information posted and easy to find before an emergency happens.

What is the normal resting heart rate for an adult horse?

28 to 44 beats per minute. A rate above 60 at rest means strong pain or trouble and is a serious emergency sign.

What is the normal respiratory rate for an adult horse at rest?

8 to 16 breaths per minute. Fast or hard breathing at rest, especially with flared nostrils or a stretched-out neck, is a breathing emergency.

What is the normal body temperature range for an adult horse?

99.5°F to 101.5°F (37.5°C to 38.6°C). Above 102°F is a fever. Above 104°F (40°C) needs an emergency call to the vet.

What is capillary refill time and what is normal?

Capillary refill time (CRT) is how long it takes for color to come back to the gums after you press them with your thumb for 2 seconds. Normal is 1 to 2 seconds. Slower than 2 seconds means poor blood flow and is a serious emergency sign.

What are the signs of colic in a horse?

Pawing, looking at or biting the belly, rolling or trying to roll, getting up and down again and again, sweating without work, not eating, a fast heart rate, and quiet or missing gut sounds. Any of these signs needs a prompt vet call — colic can get worse fast.

Which types of wounds require immediate veterinary contact?

Wounds near a joint, deep wounds, heavy bleeding with bright red blood that pulses, puncture wounds (especially in the foot), wounds on the eye or eyelid, and any wound gaping open enough to need stitches.

Why should hydrogen peroxide not be used routinely on horse wounds?

Hydrogen peroxide damages healthy tissue and can slow healing. For most wounds, the better choice is to rinse with clean water or saline, cover with sterile gauze, bandage to protect, and wait for the vet.

What is choke in horses, and what are its signs?

Choke is when food gets stuck in the esophagus (the tube to the stomach). Signs include feed or saliva coming from the nose, stretching the neck and swallowing over and over, and looking distressed. Choke needs a vet, because the horse can breathe food into the lungs if it goes on too long.

Key Vocabulary

Capillary refill time (CRT)

How long it takes for color to come back to the gums after you press them and let go. Press a thumb on the gum for 2 seconds, then time how fast the color returns. Normal is 1 to 2 seconds. A slow refill means poor blood flow.

Colic

A general term for belly pain in horses. It has many causes, such as gas, a blockage, or a twist in the gut. It can be mild or life-threatening. Always have colic checked promptly, because it is hard to tell how bad it is from the outside and it can change fast.

Gut sounds

The gurgling and rumbling sounds a healthy gut makes, heard with a stethoscope on the horse's side (flank). Quiet or missing gut sounds can be a sign of colic. Very loud sounds can mean gas colic.

Laminitis

Painful swelling inside the hoof. It can be set off by illness, too much rich feed, or other health problems. Warning signs include a strong digital pulse in the feet, hot hooves, and a horse that does not want to put weight on its feet or moves with short, careful steps. Laminitis is an emergency.

Arterial bleeding

Bleeding from an artery. The blood is bright red and pulses or spurts with the heartbeat. It needs an immediate vet call and firm, steady pressure while you wait. Do not take off a pressure bandage once it is on — that can break the clot.

Puncture wound

A wound from a sharp object going deep into the tissue. The opening can look small but hide serious damage underneath. Punctures in the foot are very serious because important joints and tendons are close to the surface and can be hit by something as small as a nail. Have a vet check any foot puncture before the object is removed.

Cohesive bandage

A self-sticking wrap (often sold as VetWrap) that sticks to itself with no tape, which makes it handy for holding dressings in place. It can give light support, but it should not be put on too tight, because a tight bandage can cut off blood flow.

Digital pulse

The pulse you can feel in the blood vessels going to the horse's foot, felt by pressing lightly at the back of the fetlock. Normally it is very hard to feel. A strong, easy-to-feel digital pulse in a horse with sore feet means extra blood flow to the foot — an early sign of laminitis or a hoof abscess.

Common Mistakes

Waiting to see if a colic episode resolves before calling the veterinarian

Some mild colic episodes resolve on their own, but the severity of a colic episode cannot always be determined from external observation. A horse that appears to be improving can have an underlying problem that is worsening internally. When a horse shows colic signs, a call to the veterinarian to describe the vital signs and behavior is always the correct first step.

Applying hydrogen peroxide or wound powders to all wounds without veterinary guidance

Hydrogen peroxide damages healthy tissue and impairs healing. Many wound powders are appropriate only for specific wound types. Applying products that damage tissue to a wound that could have healed cleanly is a common mistake. The safest approach for most wounds is to flush, cover with sterile gauze, bandage, and call the veterinarian.

Removing an object from a penetrating wound before veterinary assessment

An object embedded in a wound — especially a foot puncture — may be plugging the wound against further contamination and bleeding. Removing the object without assessing the depth, direction, and structures involved can cause significant additional injury. Objects embedded in wounds should be left in place and the wound protected until the veterinarian assesses it.

Not knowing normal vital signs before trying to assess a sick horse

A heart rate of 40 bpm cannot be identified as normal or elevated unless the handler knows the normal range. All four vital sign categories — heart rate, respiratory rate, temperature, and capillary refill time — have normal ranges that must be known before they can be used diagnostically. Learning these ranges as part of first aid training is essential.

Applying a leg bandage too tightly

A bandage applied with too much tension constricts blood flow and can cause a "bandage bow" — pressure damage to the superficial digital flexor tendon. Leg bandages should always be applied over adequate padding (quilts or combine rolls), with even, moderate tension — snug but not constricting. If a horse is uncomfortable, repeatedly picking up a bandaged leg, or shows swelling above or below the bandage, the bandage should be removed and reapplied or the veterinarian notified.

Horse Bowl Focus

What is the normal resting heart rate range for an adult horse?

28 to 44 beats per minute. A rate above 60 bpm at rest is a serious emergency indicator.

What is the normal respiratory rate for an adult horse at rest?

8 to 16 breaths per minute at rest.

What is the normal body temperature for an adult horse?

99.5°F to 101.5°F (37.5°C to 38.6°C). A temperature above 102°F is considered a fever.

What is normal capillary refill time for a horse, and how is it measured?

1 to 2 seconds. Measured by pressing a thumb against the gum above the upper teeth for 2 seconds, releasing, and timing how long it takes for the white mark to return to pink.

What is the term for esophageal obstruction in horses, and what are its signs?

Choke. Signs include feed material or saliva draining from the nostrils, repeated neck stretching and swallowing motions, and visible distress.

Why is it dangerous to remove an embedded object from a horse's foot before veterinary assessment?

The object may be penetrating structures such as the coffin joint, navicular bursa, or flexor tendon sheath, and removing it without knowing depth and direction can cause additional injury or contamination. The object should be protected in place until the veterinarian assesses it.

What type of wound bleeding indicates arterial involvement?

Bright red blood that pulses rhythmically with the heartbeat. Arterial bleeding requires immediate veterinary attention and sustained direct pressure while waiting.

What is a "bounding" digital pulse and what does it indicate?

A digital pulse that is easily felt — more prominent than normal — on palpation of the fetlock region. A bounding digital pulse in a horse showing signs of foot discomfort indicates increased blood flow to the foot, which is an early sign of laminitis or foot abscess.
