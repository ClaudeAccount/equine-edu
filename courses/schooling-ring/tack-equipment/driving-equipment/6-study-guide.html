Harness Parts

What is the collar, and what does it do?

The collar sits around the horse’s neck and shoulders and is the primary point where the horse’s pulling power transfers to the vehicle. It must be fitted correctly — too tight restricts breathing and movement; too loose allows shifting and rubs.

What are the traces, and how do they connect to the vehicle?

The traces are the long straps or chains that run from the collar or breastcollar on each side back to the vehicle (cart, carriage, or implement). They transmit the horse’s pulling force to the load.

What is the breeching, and when does it come into use?

The breeching is the strap-and-breastpiece assembly around the hindquarters. It allows the horse to push backward against the vehicle to slow it or hold it back going downhill. Without breeching, the vehicle could push into the horse’s heels on descents.

What is the crupper, and what does it prevent?

The crupper is a loop that passes under the tail at the dock and connects back to the saddle pad or backband. It prevents the harness from sliding forward toward the collar.

Bridle and Driving-Specific Parts

What are blinkers (or blinds), and why are driving horses often fitted with them?

Blinkers are rigid side panels on the driving bridle that block the horse’s rear and side vision. They keep the horse from seeing the vehicle moving behind it, which can be startling, and help the horse focus forward.

What is an overcheck or checkrein, and what does it do?

An overcheck is a rein that runs from the bit up and back over the poll to the harness, holding the horse’s head at a set height. It prevents the horse from lowering its head so far that it steps over the reins or traces.

What is the surcingle or saddle pad (driving), and where does it sit?

The surcingle or driving saddle pad sits on the horse’s back and serves as a central anchor point for several harness components, including the backband that supports the shafts.

Scenario Spot-Checks

A horse is being harnessed and the breeching seems very loose. What problem could this cause?

Loose breeching cannot effectively hold back the vehicle. Going downhill or stopping, the vehicle would push forward into the horse rather than the horse bearing the load through the breeching. The breeching should be snug enough to be immediately effective when the horse pushes back.

A driver notices the crupper has come unhooked mid-drive. What is likely to happen?

Without the crupper, the harness can shift forward toward the collar over the horse’s back as the horse moves. This changes the balance and fit of the entire harness and can cause rubbing, discomfort, and interference with driving.
