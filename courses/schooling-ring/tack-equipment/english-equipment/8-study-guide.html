Saddle Parts

Name the main parts of an English saddle.

Pommel (front arch), cantle (back arch), seat, flap, knee roll, stirrup bar, stirrup (iron), stirrup leather, girth, panels, and gullet channel.

What is the difference between the pommel and the cantle?

The pommel is the raised arch at the front of the English saddle. The cantle is the raised arch at the back. Together they frame the seat.

What does the saddle tree do, and what are the two main tree types?

The saddle tree is the rigid or semi-rigid internal frame that gives the saddle its shape and keeps the rider’s weight off the horse’s spine. The two main types are spring tree (more flexible, closer contact) and rigid tree (traditional, more supportive).

What are the panels, and why do they matter?

The panels are the padded underside of the saddle that contact the horse’s back. They distribute the rider’s weight. Proper fit of the panels is critical — poorly fitted panels can cause pain and gait changes.

Bridle Parts

Name the main parts of an English bridle from top to bottom.

Crownpiece (over the poll), browband (across the forehead), cheekpieces (down the side of the face), noseband (around the nose), throatlatch (under the jaw), bit (in the mouth), and reins.

What is the throatlatch on a bridle, and how should it be fitted?

The throatlatch is the strap that runs under the horse’s throat from the crownpiece to help keep the bridle in place. It should be fitted with enough room to fit a hand’s width flat between the throatlatch and the jaw.

What is the function of the noseband?

The noseband sits around the nose and can serve several functions depending on type: keeping the mouth closed, discouraging crossing the jaw, or simply as a conventional fitting element of English tack.

Scenario Spot-Checks

A rider says their saddle’s flocking has compressed unevenly, causing one panel to sit lower than the other. What part of the saddle are they describing, and why does it matter?

The panels are the padded underside contact surfaces of the saddle. Uneven panels create uneven weight distribution on the horse’s back, which can cause soreness, muscle asymmetry, and behavioral resistance.

A bridle is missing the browband. What is lost, and what problem might result?

The browband sits across the forehead and keeps the crownpiece from sliding backward toward the ears. Without it, the crownpiece could shift back, putting the bit in the wrong position and causing discomfort.
