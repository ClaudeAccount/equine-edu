Introduction to Tack & Equipment

The Saddle

The saddle is one of the most important pieces of riding tack — it spreads the rider's weight across the horse's back and gives the rider a secure place to sit.

The saddle is one of the most important pieces of riding tack. It helps distribute the rider's weight across the horse's back and gives the rider a secure place to sit.

A properly fitted saddle helps keep both the horse and rider comfortable. A poorly fitted saddle can cause soreness, discomfort, and problems with movement.

Why Saddle Fit Matters

The saddle must fit the horse correctly. Two important parts of saddle fit are:

  • Tree width — how wide the saddle is across the horse's shoulders and back
  • Tree angle — how the front of the saddle matches the angle of the horse's shoulders

If either of these areas does not fit correctly, the saddle can create pressure points and make the horse uncomfortable.

💡 Did You Know

The earliest known saddles were used more than 1,500 years ago. Saddles have changed over time, but their main job remains the same: helping riders sit safely and comfortably while protecting the horse's back.

Saddle Anatomy
Part 1

The Tree

The tree is the saddle's internal frame. It is usually made from wood, fiberglass, or synthetic materials. The tree gives the saddle its shape and helps distribute the rider's weight across the horse's back. Everything else in the saddle is built around the tree.

Part 2

The Seat

The seat is the part of the saddle where the rider sits. A properly sized seat helps the rider stay balanced and secure. A seat that is too large or too small can make riding uncomfortable and affect the rider's position.

Part 3

Western and English Saddles

Both Western and English saddles are designed to help riders communicate with and balance on a horse, but they look different.

Western saddles:

  • Larger and heavier
  • Have a horn on the front
  • Built for long hours of riding and ranch work

English saddles:

  • Lighter in weight
  • No horn
  • Allow closer contact with the horse

No matter the style, proper fit is important.

Part 4

Signs of Good Saddle Fit

A well-fitted saddle should:

  • Sit level on the horse's back
  • Leave space between the withers and the pommel
  • Avoid pressing on the spine
  • Make even contact along the back

After riding, sweat patterns should appear even on both sides. Uneven sweat marks can be a sign that the saddle does not fit correctly.

Saddle fit must be checked for the horse, not just the rider. Uneven sweat marks after a ride are a reliable sign that something does not fit correctly.

Saddle Fit and Horse Comfort

Many problems that seem like behavior issues can actually be caused by saddle discomfort. Signs of possible saddle fit problems include:

  • Sensitivity during saddling
  • Tail swishing or wringing
  • Reluctance to move forward
  • Bucking or resistance under saddle

When these signs appear, saddle fit should always be considered as a possible cause.

Things to Remember

  • The saddle helps distribute the rider's weight across the horse's back.
  • The tree is the saddle's internal frame and determines its shape and fit.
  • The seat is where the rider sits.
  • Western saddles have a horn; English saddles do not.
  • A properly fitted saddle helps keep the horse comfortable and moving freely.
  • Poor saddle fit can lead to soreness, muscle problems, and unwanted behaviors.
