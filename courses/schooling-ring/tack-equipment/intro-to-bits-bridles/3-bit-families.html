Intro to Bits & Bridles

Bit Families

Almost every bit is a snaffle or a curb. This lesson shows what each kind looks like and how to tell them apart by sight.

Two Main Kinds of Bits

Almost every bit is one of two kinds: a snaffle or a curb. The difference is easy to see once it is pointed out. On a snaffle, the reins attach to a ring right beside the mouthpiece. On a curb, the reins attach to a metal arm called a shank that hangs below the mouthpiece.

Learning to spot which kind a bit is the first and most useful step in understanding bits. This lesson keeps to that goal: what each kind looks like, and where it is commonly used.

💡 Did You Know

A bit is called a snaffle because of how the reins attach, not because of the shape of the mouthpiece. If the reins connect straight to a ring beside the mouthpiece, it is a snaffle — even when the mouthpiece is solid.

The snaffle is the most common bit. It is the kind most riders and horses meet first.

What a Snaffle Looks Like

A snaffle has a ring on each side of the mouthpiece, and the reins attach straight to those rings. When the rider closes the reins, the snaffle presses gently and directly on the horse's mouth. The signal is simple and easy for a horse to understand.

Because the signal is simple, snaffles are the usual choice for young horses and for everyday riding. The rings come in a few shapes — round rings that move freely, fixed rounded rings, and D-shaped rings — but they all work the same simple way.

A snaffle is the simple, direct bit: the reins attach to a ring beside the mouthpiece, and the bit presses straight on the mouth.

A curb bit sends a stronger signal than a snaffle, so it is used on trained horses and ridden with quiet, experienced hands.

What a Curb Looks Like

A curb bit has shanks — metal arms that hang below the mouthpiece. The reins attach at the bottom of the shanks instead of beside the mouthpiece. This is the easiest way to tell a curb from a snaffle: if a bit has shanks hanging down, it is a curb.

A curb bit also uses a curb chain or strap that sits under the horse's chin. Because a curb gives a stronger signal, it is meant for horses that are already well trained and for riders with soft, steady hands.

If a bit has shanks below the mouthpiece and a chain or strap under the chin, it is a curb — a stronger bit for trained horses and experienced hands.

The mouthpiece is the part of the bit that rests inside the horse's mouth. Both snaffles and curbs can have different mouthpieces.

Solid and Jointed Mouthpieces

Some mouthpieces are a single solid bar, either straight or gently curved. Others have a joint in the middle so the bar can bend. A jointed mouthpiece folds slightly when the reins are used, while a solid one stays in one piece.

The shape of the mouthpiece changes how the bit feels to the horse. At this stage, the useful skill is simply noticing whether a mouthpiece is solid or jointed.

The mouthpiece is the part that sits in the mouth. The first thing to notice is whether it is solid or has a joint in the middle.
In the Barn

Checking a Bit for Fit and Safety

A bit needs to be the right width for the horse. It should reach a little past the lips on each side — wide enough not to pinch, but not so wide that it slides across the mouth. A snaffle usually sits so that it makes one or two small wrinkles at the corners of the lips.

Bits should be checked often for rough edges, cracks, or damage. A bit with a sharp edge or a broken joint should not be used, because it can hurt the horse's mouth. Rinsing the bit after riding keeps it clean and smooth.

Things to Remember

  • Almost every bit is either a snaffle or a curb.
  • On a snaffle, the reins attach to a ring right beside the mouthpiece, and it presses straight on the mouth.
  • On a curb, the reins attach to shanks that hang below the mouthpiece, and it uses a curb chain under the chin.
  • Snaffles give a simple signal and suit young horses and everyday riding; curbs are stronger and used on trained horses.
  • The mouthpiece is the part that rests in the mouth — some are solid and some have a joint.
