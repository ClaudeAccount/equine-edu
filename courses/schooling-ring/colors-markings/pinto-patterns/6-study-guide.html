Pattern Types

Describe the tobiano pinto pattern.

Tobiano has white that typically crosses the topline (back) between the withers and tail. The legs are often white, and the head is usually solid. The edges of the white patches tend to be regular and rounded.

What is overo, and why is it described as a broad group?

Overo is a term for pinto patterns where white typically does NOT cross the topline and often originates from the belly upward. It is broad because it includes several distinct genetic patterns (frame overo, sabino, splash white) that all visually tend to leave the topline solid.

What does ‘tovero’ mean?

Tovero is a term for horses that show characteristics of both tobiano and overo patterns — for example, tobiano topline crossing combined with overo-type facial coverage or belly markings.

What is frame overo, and why does it carry a specific warning for breeders?

Frame overo has colored borders framing a white patch on the body, with the topline solid. Two copies of the frame overo gene (homozygous) produces lethal white syndrome in foals, which is fatal. Breeders should test frame overo horses before breeding.

Identification Tips

What is the fastest single clue for identifying tobiano?

White crossing the topline. If white is present above the spine between the withers and tail, tobiano is the most likely starting term.

What is splash white, and what does it typically look like?

Splash white is an overo-type pattern where white appears to have been poured from below — white legs, belly, and face (often with blue eyes). The horse looks as if the lower half was dipped in white.

Why is pattern description strongest when paired with visible evidence?

Because many horses show overlapping pattern traits, and the terms are imprecise enough that stating what is actually visible (white crosses topline, white originates from belly, etc.) communicates more accurately than the label alone.

Scenario Spot-Checks

A horse has white crossing over its back between the withers and tail, white legs, and a solid-colored head. What pattern term applies?

Tobiano. The topline crossing and white legs with a solid head are the defining tobiano indicators.

A horse has a large white patch on the lower body and flank that does not cross the topline, a blaze, and blue eyes. What pattern term is most likely?

Overo — likely splash white or frame overo. The belly-origin white that does not cross the topline and the prominent facial markings are overo-type indicators.
