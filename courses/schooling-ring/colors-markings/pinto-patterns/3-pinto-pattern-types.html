Pinto Patterns

Pinto Pattern Types

Compare common pinto pattern families through practical visual clues.

Pattern Families Can Overlap

Real horses do not always fit one category perfectly. Use the clues below as a guide, then describe the visible evidence: where the white is, whether it crosses the topline, how the edges look, and whether the horse has extra face or eye markings.

Tobiano is often the easiest pinto family for new learners to spot because the white commonly crosses the topline.
Topline Clue

Tobiano

White patches usually cross the topline between the withers and tail. Edges are often rounded or distinct.

Leg Clue

White Legs

Tobianos commonly have white on the legs, often below the knees and hocks or higher.

Head Clue

Typical Face Markings

The head may look more solid, with familiar markings such as a star, strip, snip, or blaze.

Tail Clue

Two-Color Tail

The tail is often two colors when white crosses through the tail area.

Overo is a broad visual and registry term. It can include several different white spotting patterns, so look for the specific clues.
Registry Group

Overo

White often has irregular or splashy edges and may not cross the topline in the same way as tobiano.

Important Pattern

Frame Overo

White tends to appear framed by darker color. This pattern is important in breeding because of lethal white overo risk.

Dipped Look

Splash White

White may look as if the horse was dipped in paint, often with a broad face marking, high leg white, belly white, and blue eyes.

Soft Edges

Sabino

Often shows leg white, face white, belly spots, roaning, ticking, or lace-like edges.

Tovero describes horses that show both tobiano and overo-type traits.
Combination

Tovero

A horse with tobiano plus one or more overo-type patterns. The result may be more extensive or mixed white markings.

Ear Clue

Dark Ear or Forehead Areas

Some toveros show dark pigmentation around the ears, forehead, eyes, or mouth.

Eye Clue

Blue Eyes

Blue eyes can appear in several pinto pattern families, especially splash white and some overo combinations.

Mixed Clues

Use More Than One Term

If the horse has tobiano and overo clues, describe both instead of forcing it into one simple label.

Some names describe where dark or white areas sit on the horse. These are helpful descriptive terms, not always separate genes.
Head Color

Medicine Hat

A mostly white horse with dark color mainly over the ears and poll area, like a small cap.

Head Color

Bonnet or War Bonnet

Dark color covers more of the head than a medicine hat, often extending around the forehead or eyes.

Body Color

Shield

A darker patch may sit across the chest, shoulder, or body on an otherwise very white horse.

Small Details

Ink Spots or Paw Prints

Small dark spots can appear inside white areas, especially on some tobiano-patterned horses.
