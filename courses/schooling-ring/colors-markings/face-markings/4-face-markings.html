Face Markings

Horse Face Markings

This lesson introduces the most common horse face markings and shows how to recognize and describe them. Real horse photos and examples help learners practice identifying each marking.

Understanding Face Markings

Horse face markings come in many shapes and sizes. Some are very common, while others are less common or have unusual shapes. In this lesson, learners will explore the face markings seen most often on horses. These include basic markings, combinations of markings, and markings with unique shapes. Use the tabs below to learn about each group.

The markings in the Common Markings section are the basic face markings. Many other markings are simply combinations or variations of these.

Forehead

Star

A star is a white marking on the forehead, usually between or above the eyes. Stars can be round, oval, diamond-shaped, uneven, large, small, or faint. No two stars look exactly alike. When describing a star, include both its shape and where it is located on the forehead.

Center of Face

Strip

A strip is a narrow white marking that runs down the center of the face. It usually begins below the forehead and follows the bridge of the nose toward the muzzle. A strip is thinner than a blaze.

Forehead to Muzzle

Blaze

A blaze is a wide white marking that runs down the center of the face. It follows the same general path as a strip but covers a much larger area. A blaze is wider than a strip but does not cover as much of the face as a bald face.

Muzzle

Snip

A snip is a white marking on the muzzle. It may appear between the nostrils, above the nostrils, or around one or both nostrils. Snips can be very small or cover a larger area of the muzzle.

Most of Face

Bald Face

A bald face is a very large white marking that covers most of the horse's face. It usually extends from the forehead to the muzzle and may spread around the eyes. Horses with bald faces often have pink skin beneath the white hair, and some have one or two blue eyes.

Combination markings happen when a horse has two or more face markings at the same time. Each marking is named on its own, then written together as one full description. These combinations are common and help describe a horse's face more clearly.

Forehead + Nasal

Star & Strip

A star and strip is when a horse has a star on the forehead and a strip that runs down the center of the face along the nasal bone. Sometimes the markings look like they connect, and sometimes they look separate depending on the horse's coat color.

Forehead + Muzzle

Star & Snip

A star and snip is a combination of a star on the forehead and a snip on the muzzle. The star sits higher on the face, while the snip appears as a small marking on the nose.

Nasal + Muzzle

Strip & Snip

A strip and snip is a strip running down the center of the face with a snip on the muzzle. These markings may look joined or separated, but they are still recorded as two separate parts.

Forehead + Nasal + Muzzle

Star, Strip & Snip

A star, strip, and snip includes three markings together: a star on the forehead, a strip down the nasal bone, and a snip on the muzzle. Each part is written separately, then combined into one full description.

Irregular markings do not follow clean or standard shapes. They can look uneven, broken, or off-center. These markings are still recorded carefully, with extra detail added to describe their shape.

Variation

Irregular Blaze

An irregular blaze is a blaze that does not run straight down the face. It may lean to one side, have uneven edges, or change width as it moves down the face.

Variation

Crooked Strip

A crooked strip does not stay in the center of the face. It may curve, bend, or move to one side instead of running straight down the nasal bone.

Variation

Interrupted Strip

An interrupted strip is not one solid line. It appears in sections, with breaks in between the white areas. Those breaks are important and should be included when describing it.

Variation

Offset Marking

An offset marking sits to one side of the face instead of the center. It is described by noting whether it shifts to the left or to the right.
