Face Marking Basics

Before learning the names of face markings, it helps to know the main parts of a horse's face. Understanding these areas makes it easier to recognize and describe markings correctly.

Face markings are named by where they appear on the horse's face. For example, a star appears on the forehead, while a snip appears on the muzzle. Learning these face regions first makes the marking names much easier to understand.

Parts of the Horse's Face

When identifying face markings, it is helpful to look at the horse's face from top to bottom. This is also the order often used when describing markings in records.

Top of Face

Poll

The top of the horse's head, located between the ears. Face markings do not usually appear here, but it is the highest point of the head.

Upper Face

Forehead

The area between the eyes and below the forelock. Stars and the upper part of some face markings appear here.

Mid Face

Face

The middle part of the horse's face, below the eyes and above the nostrils. Strips and blazes often run through this area.

Lower Face

Muzzle

The soft lower part of the face that includes the nostrils and upper lip. Snips are found on the muzzle.

Soft Tissue

Nostrils

The openings the horse uses for breathing. Some snips may extend around or between the nostrils.

Lower Soft Tissue

Lips & Chin

The lower part of the face below the nostrils. Large markings, such as wide blazes or bald faces, may extend into this area.

💡 Did You Know

Many horses have the same types of face markings, but no two markings are exactly alike. The size, shape, and location help make each horse unique.

Reading a Horse's Face

1

Start at the Top

Begin at the poll and forelock. Notice where the horse's natural coat color ends and any white markings begin.

2

Check the Forehead

Look at the area between the eyes. White markings here are often called stars.

3

Look Down the Face

Follow the center of the face toward the nostrils. Narrow markings are usually strips, while wider markings may be blazes.

4

Examine the Muzzle

Look closely at the muzzle and around the nostrils. White markings in this area are called snips.

5

Notice the Full Marking

Pay attention to how large the marking is and where it reaches. A complete description includes the full size and location of the marking.

Three Main Areas to Remember

Forehead

The area between the eyes where star markings appear.

Face

The center portion of the face where strips and blazes are usually found.

Muzzle

The lower part of the face where snips appear.
