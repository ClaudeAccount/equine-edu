Key Concepts

What are the common face markings (narrowest to widest)?

Face markings are described from the smallest to the widest:

  • Star — a small, separate white spot on the forehead
  • Strip — a narrow line running down the center of the face
  • Blaze — a wide white stripe down the middle of the face
  • Snip — a small white mark on the muzzle between or around the nostrils
  • Bald Face — a very wide marking that may cover most or all of the face, including the eyes and muzzle
What is a combination marking, and how is it written?

A combination marking happens when a horse has more than one face marking at the same time. Each marking is named separately and written together in order. For example: Star and Strip. It is not written as one made-up word.

What makes a marking ‘irregular,’ and how is it described?

An irregular marking does not follow a clean, even shape. It may look uneven, broken, crooked, or off-center. You describe it exactly as it appears, because those details help identify the horse.

In what direction are face markings described?

Face markings are always described top to bottom. Start at the forehead or poll area and move down the face to the muzzle and chin.

Width and Location

What is the difference between a blaze and a strip?

The difference is width. A strip is narrow and runs down the center of the face. A blaze is much wider and covers more of the front of the face. Both go vertically down the face, but a blaze takes up more space.

Where does a snip appear, and how is it different from a star?

A snip is found on the soft part of the muzzle, between or around the nostrils. A star is found on the forehead. They are in different locations and are always listed separately, even if the same horse has both.

What is a bald face, and what makes it different?

A bald face is a very wide face marking. It may cover most of the face, reach around one or both eyes, and extend all the way down to the muzzle. It is the widest type of face marking.

Scenario Spot-Checks

A horse has a small white patch on its forehead and another small patch between its nostrils. How is this described?

This is a Star and Snip combination marking. Each marking is named separately: star + snip.

A horse has a very wide white marking that runs from between the eyes down to and around the muzzle. What is it?

This is a Bald Face. The marking is wide enough to cover most of the face, including the muzzle area.
