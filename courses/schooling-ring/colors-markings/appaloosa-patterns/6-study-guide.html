Pattern Types

What is a blanket pattern, and where is the white located?

A blanket is a white or light-colored area sitting over the hip and rump. It may be solid white or contain spots (spotted blanket). The front of the horse typically retains the base coat color.

What is a leopard Appaloosa?

A leopard Appaloosa has dark spots distributed across a white or light base coat over much or all of the body. The spots are round to oval and may vary in size.

What makes varnish roan distinctive, and how can it change over time?

Varnish roan has a mix of light and dark hairs, but the darker areas (varnish marks) tend to be located at bony prominences: face, hips, knees, hocks. The coat can lighten progressively with age while the varnish marks remain darker.

What are the characteristics of a snowflake Appaloosa?

A snowflake pattern has white spots on a darker base coat. The spots tend to be small and may increase in number and size as the horse ages.

Leopard Complex and Identification

What is the leopard complex, and what is its significance for Appaloosa patterns?

The leopard complex (LP gene) is the genetic foundation of Appaloosa spotting. All Appaloosa coat patterns are expressions of this gene, which also produces mottled skin, striped hooves, and visible sclera (white around the eye).

A horse has a solid body color but mottled skin around the muzzle and eyes, and visible white sclera. Could it still be an Appaloosa?

Yes. Characteristics of the leopard complex include mottled skin, striped hooves, and white sclera even when the coat pattern is minimal or absent. A ‘few spot leopard’ or ‘near-solid’ horse can carry the LP gene without a dramatic coat pattern.

Why might an Appaloosa’s pattern not fit neatly into one category?

Real horses often show characteristics of more than one pattern category, or fall between categories. The goal is to use the closest term and describe the visible evidence rather than forcing an exact label.

Scenario Spot-Checks

A horse has white over its rump and hindquarters with dark spots inside the white area, and a solid-colored front body. What pattern is this?

Spotted blanket. The white is confined to the rump area (blanket) but contains spots within the white.

A horse appears roan but with darker patches over the hips and face. The darker areas stayed consistent while the rest lightened. What pattern term applies?

Varnish roan. The darker marks over bony prominences (varnish marks) are characteristic of this pattern.
