The Three Base Colors

What are the three base coat colors, and what pigments produce them?

Chestnut (red/phaeomelanin only), black (eumelanin only), and bay (both eumelanin and phaeomelanin — red body, black mane, tail, and points). All other horse coat colors are modifications of these three.

Describe a chestnut horse's coat, mane, and tail.

Red to reddish-brown body with a mane and tail that matches the body or is flaxen (pale cream). There are no black points. Shades range from light sorrel to dark liver chestnut, but the mane is never black.

Describe a bay horse's coat, mane, and tail.

Red-brown to dark brown body with a black mane, black tail, black lower legs (to the knee/hock), and black ear tips. The black areas are called black points. Shades range from light (blood bay) to very dark (dark bay or brown).

Describe a black horse's coat, mane, and tail.

Uniformly black body, mane, and tail with no warm (brown or reddish) tones. True black can fade to brown in summer sun — check protected areas like under the mane for the true color.

Common Mix-Ups

What is the single fastest way to separate chestnut from bay?

Look at the mane. A black mane means bay. A red or flaxen mane means chestnut. The mane color never lies.

A horse has a very dark brown body and a dark brown mane. Is this black or bay?

Likely bay (dark bay or brown). True black has no brown or warm tones even in protected areas. A dark bay can look nearly black but will show brown near the eyes, muzzle, and flanks.

What is liver chestnut, and what makes it confusing?

Liver chestnut is a very dark chestnut — deep reddish-brown or near-chocolate body. It is sometimes mistaken for black or bay. The key: liver chestnut has a matching dark mane and tail with no black, while bay always has a black mane.

What are the two pigments in horses, and what color does each produce?

Eumelanin produces black/brown coloring. Phaeomelanin produces red/yellow coloring. Every horse coat is some combination or modification of these two.

Scenario Spot-Checks

A horse has a golden-yellow body with a white mane and tail. Is this a base coat color?

No — this is palomino, a dilute color. Palomino results from a dilute gene acting on a chestnut base. The base coat colors are chestnut, black, and bay only.

A horse’s coat looks black on top but reveals reddish-brown coloring under the mane and along the flank. What is the most accurate color term?

Dark bay or brown. The reddish-brown in protected areas confirms this is bay, not true black. True black stays uniformly black even in areas unexposed to sun.

Ready Check

Ready for the quiz when all three base colors can be described from memory (body, mane, tail, points), the two-pigment rule is clear, and both scenario horses are identified correctly without peeking.
