Common Horse Colors

Study Guide

These reveal prompts are for self-testing. Trying each question before opening the answer is what makes it work. Coming back on a second day helps even more.

How to use this guide: try to answer each question before revealing the answer. Answering first, even with a guess, helps the idea stick. Use this guide twice, on different days.

Color Identification

What is the first thing to check when naming a horse's coat color?

Start with the body color, then check the mane and tail, then the lower legs. These three together usually point to the right color name.

What is the main difference between roan and gray?

Both look like a mix of colored and white hairs. A roan stays about the same for life and keeps a solid-colored head. A gray slowly lightens over time and can turn white all over, even the head.

Name the color groups covered in this course.

Base coat colors (chestnut, black, bay), golden colors (palomino, buckskin), gray, roan, and patterned coats (pinto, Appaloosa).

What are black points, and which base coat color has them?

Black points are a black mane, tail, and lower legs. Bay horses have black points. Chestnuts do not.

Common Mix-Ups

How can a dark gray be told apart from a black horse?

Check the hair roots and any spots out of the sun (under the mane, inside the legs). True black has no warm or brown tones anywhere. Gray horses show white or mixed hairs at the roots and around the face, even when still very dark.

A horse has a reddish body with a flaxen (pale cream) mane and tail. What color is it?

Chestnut. A flaxen mane and tail is a kind of chestnut, not its own color. What matters is that the mane and tail are not black.

What separates a palomino from a chestnut?

A palomino has a gold body with a white or cream mane and tail. A chestnut has a red body with a matching or flaxen mane and tail, and no gold tone.

Scenario Spot-Checks

A horse has a red-brown body, a black mane and tail, and black lower legs. What is its base color?

Bay. The black mane, tail, and legs (black points) on a red-brown body make it bay.

Someone calls a horse ‘white.’ What is the color most likely to be, and what should be checked?

Most horses called white are really gray. Check the skin under the coat (gray horses have dark skin) and look for any colored hairs left. Truly white horses are rare.
