Head and Neck Region

Name three structures in the head region covered in this course.

Any three from: poll, forelock, ear, eye, orbit, nostril, nasal bone, muzzle, chin groove, jaw, cheek, facial crest, infraorbital foramen.

Where is the throatlatch, and which two regions does it border?

The throatlatch is the area where the jaw meets the top of the neck. It appears in both the Head region and the Neck & Shoulder region because it marks the boundary between them.

What is the atlas, and where is it?

The atlas is the first cervical vertebra, located directly behind the poll. It is the joint that allows the head to nod up and down.

Body and Leg Structures

Name three structures in the body region.

Any three from: withers, back, loins, croup, point of hip, flank, barrel, ribs, heart girth, elbow point.

What is the chestnut on a horse’s leg, and where is it found?

The chestnut is a small, hard, horny growth on the inside of the leg. On front legs it is above the knee; on hind legs it is below the hock. It is a vestigial structure, a remnant of an ancestral toe.

What is the ergot?

The ergot is a small horny growth on the back of the fetlock joint. Like the chestnut, it is vestigial and found on nearly all horses, though sometimes hidden by feathering.

What lower-leg structures appear on all four legs?

Cannon bone, fetlock joint, pastern, coronet band, and hoof appear below the knee (front) or hock (hind) on all four legs.

Scenario Spot-Checks

A veterinarian mentions an injury to the ergot. What structure is affected, and where is it?

The ergot is the vestigial horny growth on the back of the fetlock joint. The vet would specify which leg.

Someone says the horse shows swelling at the point of hip. Where on the horse’s body is this?

The point of hip (tuber coxae) is the protruding bony landmark at the side of the croup, visible as the widest point of the hindquarters when viewed from above.
