Grooming Horses

Grooming Technique

Safe grooming follows a simple order. Using the same steps every time keeps the horse clean, healthy, and safe. It also helps the handler stay in the right spot around the horse.

Follow the Same Steps

Grooming works best when it follows the same order each time. Starting at the neck and moving back toward the hindquarters makes sure no part of the horse is missed. After the body comes the legs, face, mane, tail, and hooves.

Keeping the same pattern also helps the handler stay in safe spots around the horse. Moving without a plan can put a person where the horse cannot see them well or may jump suddenly.

💡 Did You Know

Horses have two blind spots — one right in front of the nose and one right behind the hindquarters. If a person steps into these spots with no warning, the horse may get scared. That is why handlers touch the neck or shoulder first before moving around the horse.

Grooming Steps

The Grooming Order, Step by Step

Step 1

Curry the body

Start at the neck and move in small circles across the shoulder, barrel (body), and hindquarters on one side. The curry comb loosens dirt, dried sweat, and loose hair. Do not use it on the legs, face, or bones — these areas are too soft.

Step 2

Dandy brush the body

Use short, quick strokes the way the hair grows. This sweeps away the dirt and hair the curry comb loosened. The dandy brush can be used on the lower legs, but press more lightly than on the body.

Step 3

Body brush the coat

Use long, smooth strokes to take off fine dust and make the coat shine. Always brush the way the hair grows. This brush works on most of the body, and on the face if used gently.

Step 4

Groom the face

Use a soft body brush or a special face brush. Move slowly around the eyes, nose, and ears. Many horses are touchy on the face, so calm, gentle brushing matters. A damp cloth or sponge can also clean around the eyes and nose.

Step 5

Detangle mane and tail

Work in small sections to take out tangles. Start at the bottom of the tail and slowly work up. This keeps hair from pulling out. Never start at the top and pull down — that breaks hair and thins the tail.

Step 6

Pick the hooves

Use a hoof pick to clean dirt, mud, and manure from each hoof. Always work from heel to toe. This step can show problems like stones, cuts, or early signs of a hoof infection.

Picking hooves is not a step to skip. Trapped dirt can lead to thrush, an infection of the frog. Stones and packed dirt can cause bruises and lameness. Pick hooves before and after every ride and every grooming session, even if the hooves look clean.

Staying Safe

Staying Safe Around the Horse

Good grooming also means standing in safe spots. Stay close to the horse instead of reaching from far away. If the horse moves fast, being close gives better balance and control. Keep one hand on the horse while the other brushes.

To switch sides, walk around the horse's head. Do not duck under the neck or walk right behind the hindquarters unless there is enough room and the horse knows where the handler is. To lift a back hoof, stand close and face toward the tail. This keeps the handler safer and steadier.

In the Barn

Horses learn routines. When grooming follows the same steps every day, most horses relax and know what comes next. If a horse suddenly reacts in one spot — like moving away, pinning its ears, or stamping — that area may be sore or tender. The reaction should be checked, not ignored.

Things to Remember

  • Always follow the same grooming order: curry → dandy brush → body brush → face → mane and tail → hooves.
  • Stay close to the horse instead of reaching far away.
  • Always start detangling the tail from the bottom.
  • Pick hooves every time, even if they look clean.
  • Walk around the horse's head when changing sides — never behind the horse without space and awareness.
