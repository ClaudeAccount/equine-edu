Grooming Horses

Grooming Technique

Safe grooming follows a simple order. Working in the same steps every time helps keep the horse clean, healthy, and safe — and helps the handler stay in the right position around the horse.

Work Through a Sequence

Grooming works best when it follows the same order each time. Starting at the neck and moving back toward the hindquarters helps make sure no part of the horse is missed. After the body, grooming moves to the legs, face, mane, tail, and hooves.

Staying in a set pattern also helps keep the handler in safe places around the horse. Moving without a plan can put a person in spots where a horse may not see them clearly or may react suddenly.

💡 Did You Know

Horses have two blind spots — one right in front of their nose and one directly behind their hindquarters. If a person enters these areas without warning, the horse may be startled. That is why handlers always touch the horse's neck or shoulder first before moving around it.

Grooming Sequence

Step-by-Step Grooming Order

Step 1

Curry the body

Start at the neck and move in small circles across the shoulder, barrel (body), and hindquarters on one side. The curry comb loosens dirt, dried sweat, and loose hair. Do not use it on the legs, face, or bones — these areas are too sensitive.

Step 2

Dandy brush the body

Use short, quick strokes in the direction the hair grows. This removes the dirt and hair the curry comb loosened. The dandy brush can be used on the lower legs, but with lighter pressure than on the body.

Step 3

Body brush the coat

Use long, smooth strokes to remove fine dust and make the coat shine. Always brush in the direction of the hair. This brush can be used on most of the body, including the face if used gently.

Step 4

Groom the face

Use a soft body brush or a special face brush. Move slowly around the eyes, nose, and ears. Many horses are sensitive on their face, so calm, gentle brushing is important. A damp cloth or sponge can also clean around the eyes and nostrils.

Step 5

Detangle mane and tail

Work in small sections to remove tangles. Start at the bottom of the tail and slowly work upward. This helps avoid pulling out hair. Never start at the top and pull down — this can break hair and thin the tail.

Step 6

Pick the hooves

Use a hoof pick to clean dirt, mud, and manure from each hoof. Always work from heel to toe. This step helps reveal problems like stones, injuries, or early signs of hoof infection.

Picking hooves is not optional. Trapped dirt can lead to thrush, an infection of the frog. Stones and debris can cause bruising and lameness. Pick hooves before and after every ride and at every grooming session, even if the hooves look clean.

Safety Positioning

Staying Safe Around the Horse

Good grooming also means staying in safe positions. Stay close to the horse instead of reaching far away. If a horse moves suddenly, being close gives better control and balance. Keep one hand on the horse while the other brushes.

When switching sides, always walk around the horse's head. Never duck under the neck or walk directly behind the hindquarters unless there is enough space and awareness. When lifting a back hoof, stand close and face toward the tail. This keeps the handler safer and more stable.

In the Barn

Horses learn routines. When grooming follows the same steps every day, most horses relax and understand what will happen next. If a horse suddenly reacts in a certain spot — like moving away, pinning ears, or stamping — it may mean that area is sore or sensitive. That reaction should be checked, not ignored.

Things to Remember

  • Always follow the same grooming order: curry → dandy brush → body brush → face → mane and tail → hooves.
  • Stay close to the horse instead of reaching far away.
  • Always start detangling the tail from the bottom.
  • Pick hooves every time, even if they look clean.
  • Walk around the horse's head when changing sides — never behind the horse without space and awareness.
