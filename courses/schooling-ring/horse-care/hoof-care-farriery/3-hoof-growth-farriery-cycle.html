Hoof Care & Farriery

Hoof Growth & the Farriery Cycle

How hooves grow, why they need trimming, and how regular farrier care keeps hooves healthy.

Hooves Never Stop Growing

Like human fingernails, a horse's hooves grow all the time, the whole of its life.

New hoof grows from the coronary band, the soft band at the top of the hoof. As new hoof grows, the older hoof moves down toward the ground.

A healthy hoof grows about 3/8 inch (1 centimeter) each month. The exact rate is a little different from horse to horse.

It usually takes 9 to 12 months for a whole new hoof wall to grow from the coronary band down to the ground.

💡 Did You Know

Hooves often grow faster in the warm months. Many horses need trimming a bit more often in summer than in winter.

Why Hooves Need Trimming

Because hooves never stop growing, they also need regular trimming.

As the hoof gets longer, its shape slowly changes. The toe usually grows faster than the heel. Over time this pulls the hoof out of balance.

If no one trims it, the toe gets too long, the hoof angle changes, and the horse has to work harder with every step.

A trim brings the hoof back to its natural balance. Then it can support the horse in comfort and work the way it should.

The Farriery Cycle

Regular hoof care follows a cycle that repeats.

As the hoof grows, the farrier trims it back to a good balance. Then the hoof grows again until the next visit.

This cycle keeps going for the horse's whole life. Most horses do best with regular visits, before the hoof gets too long or loses its shape.

How Often Should Hooves Be Trimmed?

Most horses are trimmed every 6 to 8 weeks. But there is no single schedule that fits every horse.

A few things change how fast hooves grow:

  • Age
  • Food
  • Exercise
  • Time of year
  • The horse's own rate of growth

Horses with fast-growing hooves or hard work may need a trim every 4 to 6 weeks. Horses with slow growth may do fine on an 8 to 10 week plan. The goal is to trim the hoof before it gets out of balance.

What the Farrier Looks For

Before trimming, the farrier looks closely at each hoof.

The farrier checks:

  • The overall hoof shape
  • Hoof angle
  • Toe length
  • Heel height
  • Hoof balance
  • Sole thickness
  • Frog health
  • Signs of cracks, disease, or injury

The farrier also compares the hoof angle with the pastern above it. This helps make sure the hoof and lower leg line up well. Every horse is different, so each trim fits that horse's own needs.

Shoes and Hoof Growth

Not every horse needs shoes. Many horses stay healthy and comfortable with trimming alone.

Some horses wear shoes to:

  • Protect the hoof from too much wear
  • Give better grip
  • Add support
  • Help manage certain hoof or leg problems

Even with shoes on, the hoof keeps growing at the same rate. At each visit, the old shoes come off, the hoof is trimmed and balanced, and new shoes go on if they are still needed.

A long toe is one of the most common hoof problems. As the toe gets longer, the hoof has to work harder to leave the ground with each step. This puts extra strain on the tendons, ligaments, and parts inside the hoof, including the navicular region. Over time, poor balance can lead to soreness, uneven wear, and lameness. Regular trimming helps prevent this by keeping the hoof balanced.

Staying on Schedule

Waiting too long between farrier visits usually makes more work for both the horse and the farrier.

Hooves that get overgrown may develop:

  • Long toes
  • Low heels
  • Flares
  • Cracks
  • Loose or shifted shoes
  • Changes in balance

A regular trimming schedule is usually easier and cheaper than fixing hooves that have grown badly out of shape.

In the Barn

One of the easiest ways to stay on schedule is to book the next farrier visit before the current one ends.

A regular schedule helps prevent overgrown hooves. It also lets small problems be found before they grow into bigger ones.

Picking out the horse's feet each day helps too. With a daily look, you can often spot changes in hoof shape, cracks, loose shoes, or uneven wear early.

Things to Remember

  • Hooves grow all through a horse's life.
  • New hoof grows from the coronary band and moves down as it grows.
  • A whole hoof wall usually takes 9 to 12 months to grow.
  • Most horses are trimmed every 6 to 8 weeks, but each horse's schedule may be different.
  • Regular trimming keeps the hoof balanced and supports healthy movement.
  • Shoes protect or support the hoof, but they do not stop it from growing.
  • A steady farrier schedule helps prevent many common hoof problems.
