Key Concepts

What are the main external parts of the hoof?

The main visible parts of the hoof are:

  • Hoof Wall – The hard outer covering that protects the hoof and supports much of the horse's weight.
  • Sole – The bottom surface of the hoof that helps protect sensitive structures inside the foot.
  • Frog – The soft, V-shaped structure in the center of the hoof that helps absorb shock, provide traction, and assist blood circulation.
  • White Line – The area where the hoof wall meets the sole.
  • Heel Bulbs – The rounded structures at the back of the hoof that help cushion impact when the foot lands.
What are the main internal parts of the hoof?

Important internal structures include:

  • Coffin Bone – The primary bone inside the hoof.
  • Navicular Bone – A small bone behind the coffin bone that helps support tendon movement.
  • Laminae – Interlocking tissues that connect the coffin bone to the hoof wall.
  • Digital Cushion – A flexible structure in the back of the hoof that helps absorb shock and support the heel.
What jobs does the hoof perform?

The hoof is responsible for several important functions:

  • Supporting the horse's weight
  • Absorbing shock during movement
  • Providing traction
  • Protecting internal structures
  • Assisting circulation in the lower limb

A healthy hoof helps the horse move comfortably and efficiently.

How does the hoof grow?

Hoof growth begins at the coronary band and moves downward toward the ground.

Key facts:

  • Hooves grow about 3/8 inch (1 centimeter) per month
  • Growth often increases during warmer months
  • A complete hoof wall usually takes 9 to 12 months to grow
Why do horses need regular trimming?

Hooves grow continuously throughout a horse's life.

Without regular trimming:

  • The toe may become too long
  • Hoof balance may change
  • Extra stress may be placed on joints, tendons, and ligaments
  • Movement may become less efficient

Most horses benefit from trimming every 6 to 8 weeks, although schedules vary.

How is it decided whether a horse should wear shoes?

The decision depends on several factors:

  • Hoof quality
  • Type of work performed
  • Ground conditions
  • Medical or corrective needs

Some horses remain healthy and comfortable barefoot, while others benefit from the protection or support that shoes provide.

What is thrush?

Thrush is a bacterial infection that affects the frog.

Common signs include:

  • A strong odor
  • Dark discharge in the frog grooves
  • Soft or damaged frog tissue

Regular hoof cleaning and clean living conditions help prevent thrush.

What is white line disease?

White line disease is an infection that affects the area where the hoof wall meets the sole.

Common signs include:

  • A stretched or crumbly white line
  • Separation of the hoof wall
  • Hollow areas beneath the hoof wall

Treatment usually involves removing damaged tissue and correcting the underlying hoof problem.

What is laminitis?

Laminitis is inflammation of the laminae inside the hoof.

Common signs include:

  • Warm hooves
  • A strong digital pulse
  • Reluctance to move
  • Short, careful steps
  • Leaning backward to reduce pressure on the front feet

Laminitis is a medical emergency and requires immediate veterinary attention.

What is navicular syndrome?

Navicular syndrome is a chronic condition involving pain in the back portion of the hoof.

Common signs include:

  • Short, choppy strides
  • Toe-first landing
  • Stumbling
  • Front-end lameness

Many horses remain comfortable with proper farrier and veterinary care.

Why is daily hoof care important?

Daily hoof care helps:

  • Remove dirt and debris
  • Prevent infections
  • Detect injuries early
  • Monitor hoof health
  • Prepare horses for farrier handling

Regular hoof inspections help identify problems before they become serious.

Key Vocabulary

Coronary Band

The soft band at the top of the hoof where new hoof wall growth begins.

Coffin Bone

The main bone located inside the hoof.

Digital Cushion

A flexible structure in the back of the hoof that helps absorb shock and support the heel.

Digital Pulse

A pulse that can be felt near the pastern or fetlock. A strong pulse may indicate inflammation within the hoof.

Frog

The V-shaped structure on the bottom of the hoof that helps absorb shock and provide traction.

Hoof Abscess

A pocket of infection trapped within the hoof that often causes sudden lameness.

Hoof Angle

The angle formed by the front of the hoof relative to the ground. Proper hoof angle helps distribute weight evenly.

Laminae

The tissues that connect the coffin bone to the hoof wall.

Navicular Bone

A small bone located behind the coffin bone that helps support tendon movement.

White Line

The area where the hoof wall meets the sole.

Common Mistakes

Thinking Hooves Do Not Need Trimming on Soft Ground

Hooves continue growing regardless of footing. Regular trimming is still necessary to maintain proper balance and shape.

Ignoring Early Signs of Hoof Problems

Small changes such as heat, odor, cracks, or mild soreness can become more serious if left untreated.

Transitioning From Shoes to Barefoot Too Quickly

Many horses need time to adjust when shoes are removed. Gradual transitions are usually more successful.

Digging Aggressively Into the Frog

The frog should be cleaned carefully, not scraped away. Excessive picking can damage healthy tissue.

Assuming Hoof Color Determines Hoof Strength

Research has not shown that dark hooves are stronger than light-colored hooves. Hoof quality depends on genetics, nutrition, management, and care.

  • Daily hoof care helps prevent and identify many common hoof problems.

  • Reluctance to move

What part of the hoof is affected by thrush?

The frog.

What part of the hoof is affected by white line disease?

The white line, where the hoof wall meets the sole.

What hoof shape may increase strain on the back of the foot?

A long toe and low heel.

In which direction should a hoof be cleaned with a hoof pick?

From heel to toe, helping remove debris from the hoof rather than pushing it deeper into the grooves.
