Hoof Care & Farriery

Daily Hoof Care

How to clean, inspect, and monitor a horse's hooves each day.

Why Daily Hoof Care Matters

Daily hoof care is one of the simplest and most important parts of horse management.

Cleaning and checking a horse's feet takes only a few minutes, but it can help prevent many common hoof problems. Regular hoof care removes dirt and debris, keeps the hoof clean, and makes it easier to notice changes before they become serious.

Many hoof conditions, including thrush, abscesses, cracks, and foreign objects, are easier to treat when they are found early.

💡 Did You Know

Many hoof problems begin with very small changes. A horse may take a slightly shorter step, shift weight more often, or show mild sensitivity before obvious lameness develops. Daily hoof care makes these early signs easier to notice.

The Daily Hoof Care Routine

A consistent routine helps keep both the horse and handler safe while making hoof inspections more effective.

Step 1

Ask for the Foot

Stand beside the horse near the shoulder when handling a front foot or near the hip when handling a hind foot.

Run a hand gently down the leg toward the fetlock and apply light pressure as a signal for the horse to lift its foot.

Once the foot is lifted, support it comfortably without gripping too tightly.

Maintain a balanced position and avoid placing the body directly underneath the horse.

Step 2

Clean the Hoof

Using a hoof pick, remove dirt, mud, manure, and stones from the bottom of the hoof.

Work from the heel toward the toe so debris is pushed out of the hoof rather than deeper into it.

Pay close attention to:

  • The grooves beside the frog
  • The groove in the center of the frog
  • The white line
  • The edges of the sole

Clean carefully around the frog without digging into the tissue.

Step 3

Inspect the Hoof

Cleaning the hoof provides an opportunity to check for changes or signs of a problem.

Look for:

  • Cracks in the hoof wall
  • Stones or foreign objects
  • Loose or missing shoes
  • Unusual odors
  • Dark discharge around the frog
  • Changes in the frog's appearance
  • Excessive wear
  • Damage to the sole

Feel the hoof for unusual heat and compare it with the other feet if something seems different. The most important observation is often a change from the horse's normal condition.

Step 4

Check the Lower Leg

While holding the foot, take a moment to examine the lower leg as well.

Look and feel for:

  • Swelling
  • Heat
  • Cuts or scrapes
  • Tender areas
  • Changes around the pastern, fetlock, or coronary band

Many leg and hoof problems are first noticed during routine daily handling.

Step 5

Know When to Ask for Help

Some hoof problems can wait for the next farrier visit, while others require prompt attention.

Contact a farrier if there are:

  • Loose shoes
  • Missing shoes
  • Worsening hoof cracks
  • Excessive hoof flaring
  • Changes in hoof balance

Contact a veterinarian if there are:

  • Signs of lameness
  • A strong digital pulse
  • Unusually warm hooves
  • Deep cuts or puncture wounds
  • Swelling around the coronary band
  • Signs of laminitis or an abscess

When in doubt, it is always safer to ask for professional advice.

Why early detection matters: small hoof problems can become larger problems if they go unnoticed. A stone trapped beneath the hoof, a developing abscess, or the early stages of thrush may be simple to manage when found quickly. Daily hoof care allows these issues to be identified before they affect the horse's comfort or soundness. Regular handling also helps horses become comfortable having their feet picked up, making hoof care, farrier visits, and veterinary examinations easier.

In the Barn

Daily hoof care is more than cleaning the feet. It is a quick health check that provides valuable information about the horse's overall condition. A few minutes spent cleaning and inspecting each hoof can help identify problems early, improve hoof health, and support long-term soundness. Consistent daily care often prevents minor issues from becoming major ones.

Things to Remember

  • Daily hoof care helps prevent many common hoof problems.
  • Use a hoof pick to remove debris from heel to toe.
  • Pay special attention to the frog grooves, white line, and sole.
  • Check for cracks, odors, discharge, heat, swelling, and foreign objects.
  • Examine the lower leg while handling the foot.
  • Contact a farrier or veterinarian if unusual changes or signs of pain are found.
  • Small problems are often easier to treat when they are discovered early.
