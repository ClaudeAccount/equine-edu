Hoof Care & Farriery

Study & Review Review

Vocabulary review, key principles, and Horse Bowl preparation for Hoof Care and Farriery.

This module reviews core vocabulary and principles from Hoof Care and Farriery for self-assessment and Horse Bowl preparation in the hoof health and veterinary science categories.

Core Vocabulary A
Term

Coronary band

The band of tissue at the top of the hoof from which hoof horn grows downward.

Term

Coffin bone (P3)

The primary weight-bearing bone inside the hoof capsule; can rotate or sink during severe laminitis.

Term

Laminae

Interlocking sheets of sensitive and insensitive tissue connecting the coffin bone to the inner hoof wall.

Term

Hoof angle

The angle of the hoof wall relative to the ground; should match the pastern angle in a correctly trimmed hoof.

Core Vocabulary B
Term

Thrush

Bacterial infection of the frog, characterized by black malodorous discharge in the sulci; caused by anaerobic, wet conditions.

Term

White line disease

Destruction of the non-pigmented horn at the white line by bacteria or fungi; requires resection of infected tissue.

Term

Digital pulse

The palpable pulse in the digital arteries of the lower leg; elevated digital pulse is an indicator of foot inflammation or laminitis.

Term

Long-toe-low-heel (LTLH)

A hoof conformation imbalance associated with delayed farriery; a major risk factor for navicular syndrome and DDFT injury.

Horse Bowl Focus: Hoof questions target (1) names and functions of external and internal hoof structures, (2) causes and recognition of thrush, white line disease, laminitis, and navicular syndrome, (3) the farriery cycle interval and why it is needed, (4) the barefoot vs. shod decision framework, and (5) daily hoof care inspection criteria.

Key Principles: (1) The hoof expands with each step — it is dynamic, not static. (2) Horn grows ≈3/8 inch per month; toes grow faster than heels. (3) 6–8 week farriery interval prevents long-toe accumulation. (4) White line = only barrier between environment and sensitive internal structures. (5) Thrush is prevented by daily picking and dry conditions. (6) Laminitis is always an emergency.
