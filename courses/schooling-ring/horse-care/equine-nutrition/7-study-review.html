Equine Nutrition

Study & Review

Vocabulary review, key principles, and Horse Bowl preparation for Equine Nutrition.

This module reviews core vocabulary and principles from Equine Nutrition, organized for self-assessment and Horse Bowl preparation in the nutrition and health management categories.

Core Vocabulary A
Term

Hindgut fermenter

An animal whose primary fiber fermentation occurs in the cecum and large intestine, after the stomach and small intestine.

Term

Non-structural carbohydrate (NSC)

Sugars and starches in feed; high NSC increases risk of laminitis and hindgut upset in susceptible horses.

Term

Henneke BCS

The Body Condition Score system (1–9 scale) used to assess a horse's fat reserves and nutritional status.

Term

Ration balancer

A low-calorie, high-protein, vitamin, and mineral supplement that corrects nutritional gaps without adding significant energy.

Core Vocabulary B
Term

Laminitis

Inflammation of the laminar tissue connecting the coffin bone to the hoof wall; associated with carbohydrate overload.

Term

Equine Gastric Ulcer Syndrome (EGUS)

Erosion of the gastric mucosa from prolonged exposure to stomach acid, most often caused by insufficient forage access.

Term

Choke

Esophageal obstruction; feed stuck in the esophagus — not the airway.

Term

Forage analysis

Laboratory testing of hay or pasture that quantifies nutritional content including protein, NSC, minerals, and digestible energy.

Horse Bowl Focus: Nutrition questions target (1) hindgut fermenter physiology, (2) why forage must come first, (3) BCS scoring and interpretation, (4) appropriate concentrate amounts per meal, (5) causes and prevention of colic, laminitis, choke, and EGUS, and (6) how to evaluate whether a horse needs a supplement.

Key Principles: (1) Forage is foundational — 1.5% BW/day minimum. (2) Assess the horse before choosing the feed. (3) Starch per meal ≤0.5 kg per 100 kg BW. (4) Transition diets gradually over 10–14 days. (5) Feed by weight, not by flake. (6) The most common nutritional error is overfeeding concentrate and underfeeding forage. (7) EGUS prevention = near-continuous forage access.
