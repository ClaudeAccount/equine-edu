Water and Feed Management

Water is the most important nutrient a horse needs every day. Good feeding only works when it is followed in a steady daily routine.

Putting Feeding Principles Into Daily Practice

Earlier lessons explained the rules of safe feeding: horses need steady routines, forage first, and slow changes when feed is adjusted.

This lesson is about what that looks like every day in the barn.

Feed management is where knowledge becomes habit. Knowing the rules is not enough if they are not followed the same way each day. A consistent routine is what keeps horses safe and healthy.

Water Requirements

Water Requirements

Water is the most important part of a horse's diet. A horse can only live a few days without water, even if it still has food.

Most adult horses need about 5 to 10 gallons of fresh water every day. This amount changes depending on weather, work, and diet.

Clean water must always be available. Horses should not have their water limited or only offered at certain times.

Water Needs

What Changes Water Needs

Temperature and Season

Hot weather makes horses drink much more water. In winter, very cold water may cause some horses to drink less, so water may need to be kept from freezing or slightly warmed.

Work and Exercise

When horses sweat during work, they lose water. After exercise, they need access to fresh water right away.

Hay vs. Grass Diet

Dry hay has almost no water in it, so horses must drink more. Fresh pasture has more moisture, so horses may drink a little less.

Illness or Not Eating Normally

If a horse suddenly drinks less water, it can be an early sign of sickness. This is especially important if the horse is also eating less or acting quiet or unusual.

Daily Routine

Feed Management in Daily Life

A feeding routine should stay the same every day. Horses do best when they know when and how they will be fed.

Most horses do well with:

  • Hay given in the morning and evening
  • Extra hay available during the day if needed
  • Grain only if required, and always in small, controlled amounts

Grain should never be given on an empty stomach. Hay should always come first or be given at the same time.

Daily Care Check

Feeding time is also a health check. Each time a horse is fed, it is important to notice:

  • Did the horse finish its hay?
  • Is the manure normal in amount and look?
  • Has the horse been drinking water normally?
  • Does the horse look normal in behavior and posture?

A horse that is standing quietly, not eating, or acting different may be showing early signs of a problem. Catching these changes early can prevent serious illness.

In the Barn

Feeding is not just about giving food. It is also one of the best times to check a horse's health.

A horse that is not eating normally, drinking less, or acting different should never be ignored. Small changes during feeding time are often the first warning signs that something is wrong.

Things to Remember

  • Horses need about 5 to 10 gallons of water every day, and more in heat or hard work.
  • Fresh water must always be available.
  • Drinking less water can be an early sign of illness.
  • Hay should always be the main part of the diet.
  • Grain should never be given on an empty stomach.
  • Feeding time is also a daily health check for the horse.
