Measuring Horses

The Measuring Process

Measuring a horse correctly takes more than just a measuring stick. The horse must stand the right way, the measurement must be taken from the correct place, and the right tools should be used. Taking a few extra moments to measure carefully gives a result that can be trusted.

Getting an Accurate Measurement

Imagine two people measuring the same horse. One measures on uneven ground while the horse is standing with one hind leg resting. The other measures on a flat surface with the horse standing square. Even though it is the same horse, the measurements may not match.

Small details make a big difference. Measuring correctly helps ensure height and weight records are accurate for everyday horse care.

💡 Did You Know

In some competitions and breed registrations, horses must be measured by an official measurer. The horse may even need to be barefoot because horseshoes can make a horse measure slightly taller.

Measuring Height

Measure at the Withers

A horse's height is always measured at the withers. The withers are the highest point of the horse's back, where the neck meets the shoulders.

The withers are used because they stay in the same place no matter where the horse holds its head. Measuring to the top of the head or neck would give different results every time the horse moved.

Key Point: Always measure height at the withers—not the head, neck, or hips.

Measuring Weight

Estimating Body Weight

Most barns do not have a large livestock scale, so many horses are weighed with a weight tape instead.

A weight tape wraps around the horse's heart girth, just behind the front legs. The measurement is then used to estimate the horse's weight. While it is not as accurate as a scale, it gives a good estimate for everyday horse care.

Weight estimates are commonly used when:

  • Choosing the correct amount of feed
  • Giving medications or dewormers
  • Tracking changes in body condition
  • Monitoring growth in young horses

Key Point: A livestock scale gives the most accurate weight, but a weight tape is a useful tool for estimating weight.

Equipment and Setup

Getting Ready to Measure

Before measuring, make sure both the horse and the equipment are ready.

Equipment

Measuring Stick

A measuring stick is the standard tool for checking height. It has a straight vertical pole and a horizontal arm that rests on the withers. Many measuring sticks also include a small level to help keep the arm perfectly straight.

Equipment

Weight Tape

A weight tape is wrapped around the horse's heart girth to estimate body weight. It should fit snugly against the horse without pulling too tightly.

Standing Correctly

For the most accurate measurement, the horse should:

  • Stand on a flat, hard surface
  • Stand square, with weight evenly on all four feet
  • Hold its head in a natural position
  • Stand quietly without moving

If the horse is standing unevenly or on soft ground, the measurement may not be accurate.

In the Barn

Most horses are measured right at the barn. A concrete aisle or driveway works well because the ground is level. Taking an extra moment to let the horse stand square before measuring will give a much more accurate result than rushing.

Things to Remember

  • Horse height is always measured at the withers.
  • The withers are the highest point of the back where the neck meets the shoulders.
  • A measuring stick is the best tool for measuring height.
  • Body weight is best measured with a livestock scale, but a weight tape provides a useful estimate.
  • Measure on a flat, hard surface with the horse standing square.
  • Careful measuring helps provide accurate information for health care, feeding, equipment fitting, and record keeping.
