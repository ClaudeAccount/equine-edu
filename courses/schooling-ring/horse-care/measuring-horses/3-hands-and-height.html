Measuring Horses

Hands and Height

Horse height is measured in hands, not feet or inches. Learning how the hand system works makes it easy to read, write, and compare horse heights.

Understanding Horse Height

Horse people around the world use hands to describe how tall a horse is. A hand is a standard unit of measurement equal to 4 inches.

Height is always measured at the withers, the highest point of the horse's back. Measurements are written using a special notation that is different from normal decimal numbers. Once the system is clear, reading horse heights becomes simple.

💡 Did You Know

The hand was originally based on the width of a person's hand. Today, one hand is always exactly 4 inches, making it a standard unit used throughout the horse world.

One hand equals four inches. Hand notation looks like a decimal, but the digit after the point counts extra inches, not a fraction of a hand.

The Measurement Unit

Understanding Hands

One hand equals 4 inches. Horse heights are written as whole hands, followed by the number of extra inches. For example:

  • 15.0 hands = 15 hands exactly
  • 15.1 hands = 15 hands 1 inch
  • 15.2 hands = 15 hands 2 inches
  • 15.3 hands = 15 hands 3 inches

The number after the decimal does not mean part of a hand. It tells how many extra inches there are.

Because one hand is only four inches, the only numbers after the decimal are:

  • .0
  • .1
  • .2
  • .3

A measurement such as 15.4 hands is incorrect because four more inches equals another full hand. That horse would be 16.0 hands.

Key Point: The decimal shows extra inches—not fractions of a hand.

Whether an animal is called a pony or a horse comes down to its measured height. The same height can also be written in inches or centimeters.

Ponies and Horses

What's the Difference?

The main difference between a pony and a horse is height. In general:

  • 14.2 hands (58 inches) and under is considered a pony.
  • Anything over 14.2 hands is considered a horse.

Some breed registries have their own rules, but this is the standard used in many competitions. Some countries also measure horses in centimeters instead of hands.

Converting Measurements

Changing Between Units

Hands to Inches

Multiply by 4

Multiply the number of hands by 4, then add the extra inches. Example: 16.1 hands → 16 × 4 = 64 inches, plus 1 = 65 inches.

Inches to Hands

Divide by 4

Divide the total inches by 4. The whole number is the hands, and the remainder becomes the extra inches. Example: 65 inches → 65 ÷ 4 = 16 hands with 1 inch left over = 16.1 hands.

Hands to Centimeters

Inches × 2.54

Convert to inches first, then multiply by 2.54. Example: 15.2 hands → 15 × 4 = 60 inches, plus 2 = 62 inches → 62 × 2.54 = 157.5 centimeters.

Key Point: Horse height may be written in hands, inches, or centimeters, depending on where it is being used.

A height number is only as good as the way it was taken. Height is read at one fixed point on the body, with the horse set up correctly.

Measuring a Horse's Height

Measuring at the Withers

Horse height is always measured at the withers, the highest point of the back where the neck meets the shoulders.

The withers stay in the same place even when the horse raises or lowers its head, making them the best place for an accurate measurement.

For the best result:

  • Stand the horse on a flat, hard surface.
  • Make sure all four feet are square beneath the body.
  • Use a measuring stick with a level arm.
  • Read the measurement at the top of the withers.

If the horse is standing unevenly or on soft ground, the measurement may not be accurate.

Key Point: Always measure at the withers with the horse standing square on level ground.

In the Barn

When reading a horse's height on registration papers or veterinary records, check that the notation makes sense. Heights such as 15.4 or 16.5 hands are not correct because there are only four inches in a hand. Knowing the notation system helps spot mistakes before they cause problems with equipment, competition entries, or records.

Things to Remember

  • One hand equals 4 inches.
  • The number after the decimal shows extra inches, not part of a hand.
  • Valid measurements end in .0, .1, .2, or .3 only.
  • Horses are measured at the withers.
  • A horse measuring 14.2 hands or under is generally classified as a pony.
  • Use a measuring stick and a level surface for the most accurate height measurement.
